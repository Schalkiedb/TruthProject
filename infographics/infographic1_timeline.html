Apostolic Constitutions — "Rest on Both Days"
The Apostolic Constitutions (c. 375–380 AD, Book VII, ch. 23) explicitly instructs Christians to "observe the Sabbath-day and the Lord's day" — calling for rest and assembly on BOTH Saturday and Sunday. This is not a fringe position; it is a canonical document of mainstream Christian practice. The Arians — a major 4th-century Christian movement — observed Saturday assemblies alongside Sunday ones, as documented by church historian Socrates Scholasticus (c. 440 AD, Book 6, ch. 8). Church historian Sozomen confirms the same. The dual-day Saturday/Sunday practice was the mainstream in most of the Christian world — Sunday-only was the Roman and Alexandrian exception.
