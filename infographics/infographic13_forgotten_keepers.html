Survived — Still Practised
The Nestorian Church of the East Verified
1st Century – Present · Mesopotamia / Persia / India / China
The Church of the East — historically called "Nestorian" (though they reject the label) — was the largest Christian church in the world by geographic extent from the 7th to the 14th century, stretching from Syria to China. The Schaff-Herzog Encyclopedia confirms they observed the Saturday Sabbath alongside Sunday. At their peak, they had 80 million members across Asia. Their 1,200 monasteries from Mesopotamia to Mongolia all observed Saturday services. The 781 AD Xi'an Stele in China — the earliest documented Christian presence in China — records a church practising what scholars identify as dual-day observance. The Assyrian Church of the East today (400,000 members) still conducts Saturday vespers as a liturgical holdover of this ancient practice.
Fate: Devastated by Tamerlane's massacres (1380s–1400s) which reduced them from ~80 million to under 1 million. The Saturday practice survived in liturgical form. Their near-total destruction by a Central Asian warlord — not by Rome — is why Western church histories rarely mention their Sabbath observance.

Fate: Survived the Islamic conquests, the Crusades, the Mongol invasions, and the Armenian/Assyrian genocide (1914–1923). Their Saturday practice continues. They were never subject to Rome's Council of Laodicea (364 AD) because they were outside Roman jurisdiction.

Today: 15–20 million members in Egypt and globally.

St. Thomas Christians / Nasrani of Kerala Verified
52 AD – 1599 AD (1,547 years of Saturday) · Kerala, India

Fate: The Synod of Diamper (1599) is the single most complete act of Sabbatarian cultural destruction in Asian history. 1,547 years of Saturday observance ended by fire — not by scripture. A remnant resisted and split (Coonan Cross Oath, 1653), forming the Malankara Church which preserved elements of the original practice. Today: 5 million+ Thomas Christians in Kerala.

Fate: The Bosnian Church (the institutional form of Bogomilism) was destroyed after the Ottoman conquest (1463). Many Bogomils converted to Islam — leading to the Muslim population of modern Bosnia. Their physical destruction was total; their theological impact survives only in the records of their persecutors.
