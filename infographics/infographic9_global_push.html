ILO / UN

ILO Convention No. 14 — Weekly Rest Since 1921

International Labour Organization · Global Standard · 187 Member States

The ILO's Weekly Rest (Industry) Convention No. 14 (1921) established a 24-hour consecutive weekly rest period as an international standard. ILO Convention No. 106 (1957) extended this to commerce and offices. Both explicitly note that "where possible" this rest day should coincide with the day established by tradition in the country concerned. In most Western nations, that tradition is Sunday — giving UN institutional backing to Sunday-specific rest without explicitly mandating it.

187 member states party to ILO. The 1921 convention note: "weekly rest primarily Sunday" is standard language in multiple national implementing laws

⚠ The Religious Liberty Concern — Three Critical Issues
The Double-Burden Problem

Any law making Sunday the "uniform rest day" places an extra economic burden on 70M+ Saturday-observing Christians, plus all Jewish (Friday sunset–Saturday), Muslim (Friday), and other faith communities. Businesses keeping their own Sabbath must effectively "close twice" — losing revenue against Sunday-only competitors. Heritage Foundation SR323 acknowledges this but dismisses it, stating Sunday is the only "realistic" choice for the majority.

The First Amendment Question

The 1961 McGowan v. Maryland ruling upheld Sunday laws only because they were framed as secular labour regulations with incidental religious effects. SR323's own text uses theological language — "God ordained the Sabbath" — that may constitute an Establishment Clause violation. Legal scholars note this is a fundamentally different constitutional situation than 1961. SDA Church, BJC, and Interfaith Alliance all have First Amendment attorneys monitoring the situation.
