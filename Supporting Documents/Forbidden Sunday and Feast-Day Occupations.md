### Page 2 (Content Reconstruction)

The Sabbath was Saturday, not Sunday. The early Christians did not transfer
the observance of the seventh day to the first, but added the observance
of Sunday to that of Saturday.

Sunday was observed because it was the day on which Christ rose from
the dead, and because of the many manifestations of Christ to His
disciples on that day. It was, therefore, regarded as the Lord’s Day.

In the early centuries, both days were observed; but gradually the
observance of the Sabbath declined, while that of Sunday increased,
until finally the Sabbath was entirely abandoned by Christians.

It was not until several centuries after Christ that the observance
of Sunday was prescribed by law, and that the obligation of keeping
holy the Sabbath day was transferred from Saturday to Sunday.

The Church, therefore, in virtue of the authority given to her by
Christ, has the power to determine the days of worship, and to
prescribe the manner in which they are to be observed.

God simply gave His Church the power to set aside whatever day
or days she would deem suitable as holy days. The Church chose
Sunday, the first day of the week, and in the course of time added
other days as holy days.
