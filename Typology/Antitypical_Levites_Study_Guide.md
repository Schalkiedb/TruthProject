# The Sons of Levi: Called to Be a Holy Priesthood
## A Typology Study — What God Required of the Levites, and What He Requires of His People Now

*All Scripture is quoted from the King James Version.*

---

## Introduction: "Who Is on the LORD's Side?"

When Israel turned to the golden calf at the foot of Sinai, Moses stood at the gate of the camp and called for a decision:

> **Exodus 32:26** — "Then Moses stood in the gate of the camp, and said, Who is on the LORD's side? let him come unto me. And all the sons of Levi gathered themselves together unto him."

That choice set the tribe of Levi apart for God's service. Moses told them, "Consecrate yourselves to day to the LORD... that he may bestow upon you a blessing this day" (**Exodus 32:29**).

The New Testament gives the same calling to every believer:

> **1 Peter 2:5** — "Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house, an holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God by Jesus Christ."

> **1 Peter 2:9** — "But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:"

Peter is quoting the calling God gave Israel at Sinai:

> **Exodus 19:5-6** — "Now therefore, if ye will obey my voice indeed, and keep my covenant, then ye shall be a peculiar treasure unto me above all people: for all the earth is mine: And ye shall be unto me a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation."

The question this study asks is simple. **If God's people are now a priesthood, what did God require of the priests and Levites, and how much of it still applies?**

The answer: everything that came from God's holiness still applies. Only what pointed forward to the cross has ended.

---

## Background: From Curse to Blessing

Levi did not start out as a model of holiness. There was nothing in him to recommend him to God, which is exactly why he is a fitting type of believers, who are called by grace.

### Levi's Sword in His Own Anger

When Shechem defiled their sister Dinah, Simeon and Levi took revenge by deceit and slaughter:

> **Genesis 34:25** — "And it came to pass on the third day, when they were sore, that two of the sons of Jacob, Simeon and Levi, Dinah's brethren, took each man his sword, and came upon the city boldly, and slew all the males."

On his deathbed, Jacob did not bless them for it. He cursed their anger:

> **Genesis 49:5-7** — "Simeon and Levi are brethren; instruments of cruelty are in their habitations. O my soul, come not thou into their secret; unto their assembly, mine honour, be not thou united: for in their anger they slew a man, and in their selfwill they digged down a wall. Cursed be their anger, for it was fierce; and their wrath, for it was cruel: I will divide them in Jacob, and scatter them in Israel."

### Levi's Sword at God's Command

At Sinai, the sons of Levi took up the sword again (**Exodus 32:26-28**). The act looked the same, but the two were opposites. At Shechem they acted "in their selfwill," by their own decision and in their own anger. At Sinai they acted on the word of the LORD, after choosing His side.

### The Curse Became the Blessing

Jacob's sentence was carried out, "I will divide them in Jacob, and scatter them in Israel," but God turned it into Levi's calling. Levi received no single territory. Instead the tribe was scattered through every other tribe, in forty-eight cities:

> **Joshua 21:41** — "All the cities of the Levites within the possession of the children of Israel were forty and eight cities with their suburbs."

The scattering that was a curse became the way God placed teachers of His law in every part of Israel.

### The Same Spirit in the Disciples

Jesus' own disciples showed Levi's old spirit. When a Samaritan village refused Him:

> **Luke 9:54-56** — "And when his disciples James and John saw this, they said, Lord, wilt thou that we command fire to come down from heaven, and consume them, even as Elias did? But he turned, and rebuked them, and said, Ye know not what manner of spirit ye are of. For the Son of man is not come to destroy men's lives, but to save them."

James and John were changed, as Levi was. That is the pattern for every believer: called as they are, and not left as they are.

## Part 1: What Ended and What Did Not

Before comparing the two, one line must be drawn clearly, because Scripture draws it clearly.

### 1.1 The Sacrifices Ended

The Levitical sacrifices were a shadow of Christ's sacrifice:

> **Hebrews 10:1** — "For the law having a shadow of good things to come, and not the very image of the things, can never with those sacrifices which they offered year by year continually make the comers thereunto perfect."

> **Hebrews 10:12** — "But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God;"

When the Lamb of God died, the shadow met its substance. No believer offers a lamb, because the Lamb has been offered. (See *Objection 5* of the Sabbath Study Guide for how Scripture itself defines a "shadow.")

### 1.2 Mediation Belongs to Christ Alone

Believers are a priesthood, but they are **not** mediators between sinners and God:

> **1 Timothy 2:5** — "For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;"

> **Hebrews 7:24-25** — "But this man, because he continueth ever, hath an unchangeable priesthood. Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them."

This is where the Levites are the right type. The Levites were **not** the high priest. They served *under* the high priest:

> **Numbers 3:6** — "Bring the tribe of Levi near, and present them before Aaron the priest, that they may minister unto him."

Aaron stood before God for the people; the Levites assisted Aaron. In the same way, Christ is our High Priest in the heavenly sanctuary (**Hebrews 8:1-2**), and His people serve under Him. They do not take His place. (See *Prophet, Priest & King — Biblical Definitions*, Implication 6, for the same point.)

### 1.3 What Did Not End

What remains is everything God required because **He is holy**: separation from sin, a clean life, obedience to His law, teaching His people the difference between holy and common, and a life given to His service. None of these were shadows of the cross. They were descriptions of what it means to belong to a holy God, and God has not stopped being holy.

## Part 2: The Parallel — Point by Point

### 2.1 Chosen and Separated

> **Numbers 8:14** — "Thus shalt thou separate the Levites from among the children of Israel: and the Levites shall be mine."

> **2 Corinthians 6:17** — "Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you,"

The Levites were taken out of the other tribes to belong to God. Paul applies the same word, *separate*, to believers, and adds "touch not the unclean thing." The final call of Revelation is the same: "Come out of her, my people" (**Revelation 18:4**).

God's call to Abraham, Levi's ancestor, had the same shape: "Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father's house, unto a land that I will shew thee" (**Genesis 12:1**). Stephen quotes those words to the people of his own day (**Acts 7:3**). Separation was part of the calling from the start.

### 2.2 Joined to the High Priest

The name Levi itself means "joined":

> **Genesis 29:34** — "And she conceived again, and bare a son; and said, Now this time will my husband be joined unto me, because I have born him three sons: therefore was his name called Levi."

God later used the same word for the tribe's relationship to Aaron:

> **Numbers 18:2** — "And thy brethren also of the tribe of Levi, the tribe of thy father, bring thou with thee, that they may be joined unto thee, and minister unto thee:"

Four steps in Numbers describe that joining, and each has a New Testament counterpart in Christ, the High Priest:

- **Taken in place of the firstborn.** "I have taken the Levites from among the children of Israel instead of all the firstborn... therefore the Levites shall be mine" (**Numbers 3:12**). Believers belong to "the general assembly and church of the firstborn, which are written in heaven" (**Hebrews 12:23**).
- **Given to the high priest as a gift.** "And I have given the Levites as a gift to Aaron and to his sons" (**Numbers 8:19**). Jesus says of His people: "thine they were, and thou gavest them me; and they have kept thy word" (**John 17:6**).
- **Presented before him.** "Bring the tribe of Levi near, and present them before Aaron the priest" (**Numbers 3:6**). Christ will "present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish" (**Ephesians 5:27**).
- **Offered as a living offering.** "And Aaron shall offer the Levites before the LORD for an offering of the children of Israel, that they may execute the service of the LORD" (**Numbers 8:11**). They were not slain; they were given to God alive. Paul describes his gospel work the same way: "that the offering up of the Gentiles might be acceptable, being sanctified by the Holy Ghost" (**Romans 15:16**).

The Levites were not taken against their will. They came first, when Moses asked "Who is on the LORD's side?" Being given to the high priest followed their choice.

### 2.3 Loyalty to God Above Family

When Moses blessed Levi, he remembered what they did at Sinai:

> **Deuteronomy 33:9-10** — "Who said unto his father and to his mother, I have not seen him; neither did he acknowledge his brethren, nor knew his own children: for they have observed thy word, and kept thy covenant. They shall teach Jacob thy judgments, and Israel thy law: they shall put incense before thee, and whole burnt sacrifice upon thine altar."

Jesus requires the same loyalty:

> **Matthew 10:37** — "He that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me: and he that loveth son or daughter more than me is not worthy of me."

**An honest note on Exodus 32**: at Sinai the Levites took up the sword against the idolaters (**Exodus 32:27-28**). That act belonged to Israel as a nation under God's direct rule. It is not the antitype. The New Testament priesthood fights with other weapons: "For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds" (**2 Corinthians 10:4**). Its sword is "the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God" (**Ephesians 6:17**), and like Levi's sword at Sinai it may only be used at God's command, never in our own anger (see the Background above). What carries over is the loyalty, not the steel.

### 2.4 The LORD Is Their Inheritance

> **Deuteronomy 10:9** — "Wherefore Levi hath no part nor inheritance with his brethren; the LORD is his inheritance, according as the LORD thy God promised him."

Levi received no tribal land. Believers likewise live as "strangers and pilgrims on the earth" (**Hebrews 11:13**), waiting for an inheritance that is not here:

> **1 Peter 1:4** — "To an inheritance incorruptible, and undefiled, and that fadeth not away, reserved in heaven for you,"

> **Hebrews 13:14** — "For here have we no continuing city, but we seek one to come."

"No inheritance" did not mean the Levites went without. They were given cities to live in and pasture for their animals (**Numbers 35:2**), and they lived from the tithe (**Numbers 18:21**). They had what they needed, but their portion was the LORD, not the land. Abraham lived the same way, as a stranger in the land of promise: "For he looked for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God" (**Hebrews 11:10**).

### 2.5 Called to Be Holy

> **Leviticus 11:44** — "For I am the LORD your God: ye shall therefore sanctify yourselves, and ye shall be holy; for I am holy: neither shall ye defile yourselves with any manner of creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth."

> **1 Peter 1:15-16** — "But as he which hath called you is holy, so be ye holy in all manner of conversation; Because it is written, Be ye holy; for I am holy."

Peter grounds the Christian's holiness in "it is written," and the words he quotes are Leviticus. The reason has not changed: "for I am holy."

**An honest note**: "Be ye holy; for I am holy" appears in several places in Leviticus (**11:44-45**, **19:2**, **20:7**, **20:26**). Leviticus 11 is the chapter on clean and unclean food, but Peter does not say which passage he is quoting, so this study does not claim he meant chapter 11 specifically. The point that stands is that Peter takes Levitical holiness and applies it directly to believers.

### 2.6 Clean and Unclean

The distinction between clean and unclean animals is older than Levi, older than Moses, and older than Israel:

> **Genesis 7:2** — "Of every clean beast thou shalt take to thee by sevens, the male and his female: and of beasts that are not clean by two, the male and his female."

Noah knew the difference before there was a Levite or a tabernacle. The food laws were not created by the sacrificial system, so they did not end with it.

Isaiah places the eating of unclean food at the very end of time, at the Lord's coming:

> **Isaiah 66:15-17** — "For, behold, the LORD will come with fire... They that sanctify themselves, and purify themselves in the gardens behind one tree in the midst, eating swine's flesh, and the abomination, and the mouse, shall be consumed together, saith the LORD."

For the common objections — Peter's vision in Acts 10 and "purging all meats" in Mark 7:19 — see the *Pork Study Guide* in the Modern Diet and Disease section, which answers both.

### 2.7 Teaching the Difference Between Holy and Common

This was one of the priests' main duties:

> **Leviticus 10:10-11** — "And that ye may put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean; And that ye may teach the children of Israel all the statutes which the LORD hath spoken unto them by the hand of Moses."

Ezekiel describes the faithful priests of the future temple with the same duty:

> **Ezekiel 44:23** — "And they shall teach my people the difference between the holy and profane, and cause them to discern between the unclean and the clean."

And he describes unfaithful priests by their failure in exactly these areas, including the Sabbath:

> **Ezekiel 22:26** — "Her priests have violated my law, and have profaned mine holy things: they have put no difference between the holy and profane, neither have they shewed difference between the unclean and the clean, and have hid their eyes from my sabbaths, and I am profaned among them."

A priesthood that no longer separates holy from common, clean from unclean, or the Sabbath from other days, is the priesthood Ezekiel condemns.

### 2.8 Obedience

At their consecration, the priests' obedience was complete:

> **Leviticus 8:36** — "So Aaron and his sons did all things which the LORD commanded by the hand of Moses."

Paul asks the same of the church:

> **2 Corinthians 2:9** — "For to this end also did I write, that I might know the proof of you, whether ye be obedient in all things."

> **John 15:10** — "If ye keep my commandments, ye shall abide in my love; even as I have kept my Father's commandments, and abide in his love."

God made a covenant with Levi, and the covenant was one of faithfulness:

> **Malachi 2:4-5** — "And ye shall know that I have sent this commandment unto you, that my covenant might be with Levi, saith the LORD of hosts. My covenant was with him of life and peace;"

> **Malachi 2:6-7** — "The law of truth was in his mouth, and iniquity was not found in his lips: he walked with me in peace and equity, and did turn many away from iniquity. For the priest's lips should keep knowledge, and they should seek the law at his mouth: for he is the messenger of the LORD of hosts."

> **John 14:15** — "If ye love me, keep my commandments."

> **Revelation 14:12** — "Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus."

### 2.9 Ministering to the LORD

> **Deuteronomy 10:8** — "At that time the LORD separated the tribe of Levi, to bear the ark of the covenant of the LORD, to stand before the LORD to minister unto him, and to bless in his name, unto this day."

The Levites had four tasks in this verse: to carry God's law (the ark held the Ten Commandments), to stand before God, to minister to Him, and to bless in His name. Each has a New Testament counterpart:

- **Carry His law** — written now on the heart (**Jeremiah 31:33**; **2 Corinthians 3:3**)
- **Stand before Him** — "Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace" (**Hebrews 4:16**)
- **Minister to Him** — "Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me" (**Matthew 25:40**)
- **Bless in His name** — "Bless them which persecute you" (**Romans 12:14**)

Hezekiah reminded the Levites of their calling when he restored the temple:

> **2 Chronicles 29:11** — "My sons, be not now negligent: for the LORD hath chosen you to stand before him, to serve him, and that ye should minister unto him, and burn incense."

> **Hebrews 6:10** — "For God is not unrighteous to forget your work and labour of love, which ye have shewed toward his name, in that ye have ministered to the saints, and do minister."

### 2.10 Carrying the Ark — God's Way

Only the Levites were to carry the ark, and only on their shoulders:

> **1 Chronicles 15:2** — "Then David said, None ought to carry the ark of God but the Levites: for them hath the LORD chosen to carry the ark of God, and to minister unto him for ever."

David learned this the hard way. His first attempt put the ark on a new cart, and Uzzah died when he reached out to steady it (**1 Chronicles 13:7-10**). David later explained what went wrong:

> **1 Chronicles 15:13, 15** — "For because ye did it not at the first, the LORD our God made a breach upon us, for that we sought him not after the due order... And the children of the Levites bare the ark of God upon their shoulders with the staves thereon, as Moses commanded according to the word of the LORD."

The ark held the Ten Commandments. The lesson for the antitypical Levite is that God's law is to be carried, not dragged, and carried in the way He commanded, not in a way that seems more convenient. Jesus describes that load:

> **Matthew 11:29-30** — "Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light."

In Egypt the tribe carried Pharaoh's burdens. In the wilderness they carried God's law. One was bondage; the other was a light burden.

### 2.11 Clean Garments

> **Exodus 28:2** — "And thou shalt make holy garments for Aaron thy brother for glory and for beauty."

> **Revelation 19:8** — "And to her was granted that she should be arrayed in fine linen, clean and white: for the fine linen is the righteousness of saints."

Zechariah shows a high priest whose filthy garments are taken away and replaced, with God explaining what that means:

> **Zechariah 3:4** — "...Take away the filthy garments from him. And unto him he said, Behold, I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee, and I will clothe thee with change of raiment."

The garments of the priesthood are now the righteousness Christ gives.

The contrast at Sinai makes the same point. After the golden calf, "Moses saw that the people were naked; (for Aaron had made them naked unto their shame among their enemies:)" (**Exodus 32:25**). Christ's counsel to the last church uses the same picture: "buy of me... white raiment, that thou mayest be clothed, and that the shame of thy nakedness do not appear" (**Revelation 3:18**).

The priests were also anointed: "Then shalt thou take the anointing oil, and pour it upon his head, and anoint him" (**Exodus 29:7**). Believers have the anointing of the Spirit: "But ye have an unction from the Holy One, and ye know all things" (**1 John 2:20**).

### 2.12 Washed Before Serving

> **Exodus 30:20** — "When they go into the tabernacle of the congregation, they shall wash with water, that they die not;"

> **Hebrews 10:22** — "Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water."

### 2.13 The Whole Person Consecrated

At their consecration, blood was placed on the priest's right ear, right thumb, and right big toe (**Leviticus 8:23-24**): what he heard, what he did, and where he went all belonged to God.

> **Romans 12:1** — "I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service."

The Greek word for "service" here, *latreia*, is the word the New Testament uses for the priestly service of the sanctuary (**Hebrews 9:1, 6**). Paul is describing the believer's body as the offering of a priest.

### 2.14 Sober and Clear-Minded

> **Leviticus 10:9** — "Do not drink wine nor strong drink, thou, nor thy sons with thee, when ye go into the tabernacle of the congregation, lest ye die: it shall be a statute for ever throughout your generations:"

> **Ephesians 5:18** — "And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;"

The very next verse gives the reason for the rule: "And that ye may put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean" (**Leviticus 10:10**). A priest had to keep a clear mind because his work was to discern.

**An honest note**: Leviticus 10:9 forbids wine "when ye go into the tabernacle," that is, while on duty. The New Testament priesthood is never off duty, which is why the principle reaches the whole of life. But this is an application of the principle, not a direct transfer of the statute.

### 2.15 Spiritual Sacrifices

Animal sacrifice has ended, but the priesthood still offers sacrifices. Peter calls them "spiritual sacrifices" (**1 Peter 2:5**), and Scripture names them:

- **Praise** — "By him therefore let us offer the sacrifice of praise to God continually, that is, the fruit of our lips giving thanks to his name" (**Hebrews 13:15**)
- **Doing good and sharing** — "But to do good and to communicate forget not: for with such sacrifices God is well pleased" (**Hebrews 13:16**)
- **A broken heart** — "The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit" (**Psalm 51:17**)
- **The body itself** — "a living sacrifice" (**Romans 12:1**)

### 2.16 The Lamp, the Incense, and the Bread

The daily duties in the holy place each have a counterpart:

- **The lamp burning always** (**Exodus 27:20**) — "Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven" (**Matthew 5:16**)
- **The incense** — "Let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense" (**Psalm 141:2**); "the smoke of the incense, which came with the prayers of the saints, ascended up before God" (**Revelation 8:4**)
- **The bread of the presence** — "Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God" (**Matthew 4:4**)

### 2.17 Keepers of the Cities of Refuge

The six cities of refuge were Levite cities:

> **Numbers 35:6** — "And among the cities which ye shall give unto the Levites there shall be six cities for refuge, which ye shall appoint for the manslayer, that he may flee thither: and to them ye shall add forty and two cities."

A manslayer who reached one was safe, but only while he stayed inside, and only until a set time: "he shall abide in it unto the death of the high priest, which was anointed with the holy oil" (**Numbers 35:25**). If he left the city before then, the avenger of blood could lawfully kill him (**Numbers 35:26-27**).

The New Testament uses the same language for the believer:

> **Hebrews 6:18-20** — "That by two immutable things, in which it was impossible for God to lie, we might have a strong consolation, who have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us: Which hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both sure and stedfast, and which entereth into that within the veil; Whither the forerunner is for us entered, even Jesus, made an high priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec."

Our High Priest is "made an high priest for ever." He never dies, so the refuge never ends, and the one who has fled to it has no reason ever to leave. The Levites kept the cities and received those who fled to them; the antitypical Levite points sinners to the refuge that is Christ.

**An honest note**: the manslayer in Numbers 35 is someone who killed "at unawares" (v. 11), not a deliberate murderer, and Hebrews 6:18 does not mention the cities of refuge by name. The connection rests on the shared language of "fleeing for refuge" and on the role of the high priest. It is a sound typological reading, but it is a reading, not a statement of the text.

## Part 4: An Objection Examined

### "Hebrews 7:12 says the law changed when the priesthood changed"

> **Hebrews 7:12** — "For the priesthood being changed, there is made of necessity a change also of the law."

Read the next four verses. The "law" that changed is the law about **who may be priest**. Under Moses, priests had to come from Levi. Jesus came from Judah, "of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood" (**Hebrews 7:14**). So the law of priestly descent had to change for Christ to be High Priest:

> **Hebrews 7:16** — "Who is made, not after the law of a carnal commandment, but after the power of an endless life."

The changed law is "the law of a carnal commandment," the rule of physical descent. It is not the Ten Commandments, and it is not God's call to holiness, which Peter repeats to the new priesthood (**1 Peter 1:15-16**).

## Part 5: The End-Time Sons of Levi

Two prophecies speak of Levites at the end of the age.

**Isaiah 66 — Priests and Levites from the nations:**

> **Isaiah 66:20-21** — "And they shall bring all your brethren for an offering unto the LORD out of all nations... And I will also take of them for priests and for Levites, saith the LORD."

The same chapter condemns those who eat swine's flesh at the Lord's coming (v. 17) and says that "from one sabbath to another, shall all flesh come to worship before me" (v. 23).

**An honest note**: commentators differ on who "them" refers to in verse 21 — the people of the nations, or the Israelites they bring back. This study does not rest anything on one reading. Either way, God promises a priesthood at the end that is not limited to the tribe of Levi.

**Malachi 3 — The sons of Levi purified:**

> **Malachi 3:3** — "And he shall sit as a refiner and purifier of silver: and he shall purify the sons of Levi, and purge them as gold and silver, that they may offer unto the LORD an offering in righteousness."

Malachi places this purifying at the coming of the Lord to His temple (**Malachi 3:1-2**). In its first setting it addresses Israel's priests; applying it to God's people now is a typological reading, consistent with Peter calling believers a priesthood. The picture is of a people refined, not destroyed, so that what they offer God is righteous.

## Part 6: The Final Priesthood

The priesthood of believers does not end at Christ's return. It continues:

> **Revelation 1:6** — "And hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father; to him be glory and dominion for ever and ever. Amen."

> **Revelation 20:6** — "Blessed and holy is he that hath part in the first resurrection: on such the second death hath no power, but they shall be priests of God and of Christ, and shall reign with him a thousand years."

## Conclusion

The question at Sinai is the question now: "Who is on the LORD's side?"

The Levites answered by stepping forward, and God gave them a life of separation, holiness, obedience, and service. Christ fulfilled the sacrifices and holds the high priesthood alone. Everything else God asked of the Levites came from His own holiness, and He still asks it of the people He calls a holy priesthood: be separate, be clean, keep His commandments, teach the difference between holy and common, and give your whole life to His service.

> **1 Peter 2:9** — "But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:"
