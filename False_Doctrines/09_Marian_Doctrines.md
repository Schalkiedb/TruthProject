# False Doctrine 9: Marian Doctrines — What Does Scripture Say About Mary?
## A Bible-Only Study

## Introduction: Honouring Mary Rightly vs. Worshipping Mary Wrongly

Mary was a remarkable woman. She was chosen by God for the most extraordinary privilege given to any human being — to carry and birth the Son of God. She is called "blessed among women" (Luke 1:28, 42). No honest Bible student would diminish her role.

But there is a profound difference between *honouring* Mary as a chosen servant of God and *elevating* her to a position Scripture never assigns to her. This study examines four specific Catholic doctrines about Mary against the only standard that matters: God's Word.

> "Every word of God is pure... Do not add to His words, lest He rebuke you, and you be found a liar." (Proverbs 30:5-6)

---

## Marian Doctrine 1: Mary as Mediatrix and Co-Redemptrix

### What the Catholic Church Teaches

**Catholic Catechism (CCC 969)**: "Therefore the Blessed Virgin is invoked in the Church under the titles of Advocate, Helper, Benefactress, and Mediatrix."

**Pope John Paul II**: Called Mary "Co-Redemptrix" and "Mediatrix of all graces" in multiple papal statements, encouraging many to petition for this to be defined as dogma.

**The Rosary**: Is addressed primarily to Mary: "Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee... pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death."

### What Scripture Teaches

There is **one Mediator** — and Scripture names him specifically:

> "For there is **one God and one Mediator between God and men, the Man Christ Jesus**." (1 Timothy 2:5)

The word **one** (*heis* in Greek) is absolute. Singular. Exclusive. There is no second mediator, no chain of intercessors, no co-mediator.

> "Therefore He is also able to save to the uttermost those who come to God through Him, since **He always lives to make intercession for them**." (Hebrews 7:25)

Christ's intercession is **continuous, present, and perfect**. There is no gap in His mediation that Mary needs to fill.

> "Jesus said to him, '**I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me.**'" (John 14:6)

**No one** — *oudeis* in Greek — comes to the Father except through Jesus. Mary is not mentioned as an alternative route.

### Mary's Own Words

Mary herself directed attention **away** from herself and **to** her Son:

> "His mother said to the servants, '**Whatever He says to you, do it.**'" (John 2:5)

Mary's instruction is: obey Jesus. Not: come to me. Not: pray through me.

Mary herself acknowledged her need for a Savior:

> "And my spirit has rejoiced in **God my Savior**." (Luke 1:47)

A person who needs saving is not a co-redeemer. A redeemer saves others; they themselves do not need saving.

**Conclusion**: The titles Mediatrix and Co-Redemptrix directly contradict 1 Timothy 2:5, Hebrews 7:25, and John 14:6. These are not extensions of biblical teaching — they are replacements of it.

## Marian Doctrine 2: The Immaculate Conception of Mary (Dogma, 1854 CE)

### What the Catholic Church Teaches

**Pope Pius IX, Ineffabilis Deus (1854 CE)**: "The most Blessed Virgin Mary, in the first instant of her conception, by a singular grace and privilege granted by Almighty God, in view of the merits of Jesus Christ, the Saviour of the human race, was preserved free from all stain of original sin."

This teaches that Mary was born without original sin — sinless from conception — by a special divine act.

### What Scripture Teaches

> "For **all have sinned** and fall short of the glory of God." (Romans 3:23)

The word **all** (*pantes* in Greek) means all human beings without exception.

> "There is **none righteous, no, not one**." (Romans 3:10, quoting Psalm 14:1-3)

> "...for there is **no one who does not sin**." (1 Kings 8:46)

> "Who can say, 'I have made my heart clean, I am pure from my sin'?" (Proverbs 20:9)

These absolute statements leave no room for any human exception apart from Jesus Christ — whose unique sinlessness is established through His divine conception (He did not have a human father), not through Mary's supposed sinlessness.

### Mary's Own Testimony

Mary called God "my Savior" (Luke 1:47). If she were conceived sinless and remained sinless, she would have no need of a Savior. The doctrine of the Immaculate Conception contradicts Mary's own words.

### Historical Note

The Immaculate Conception was not the universal teaching of the early church. Even Aquinas (13th century) and Bernard of Clairvaux opposed it. It was only formally defined as dogma in 1854 CE — a declaration made by a pope on his own authority, without a council, citing his own infallibility.

**Conclusion**: The Immaculate Conception of Mary has no biblical support and directly contradicts the universal statements "all have sinned" and Mary's self-identification as needing a Savior.

## Marian Doctrine 3: The Perpetual Virginity of Mary

### What the Catholic Church Teaches

**Catholic Catechism (CCC 499-501)**: Mary remained a virgin before, during, and after the birth of Jesus — perpetually and permanently. The "brothers and sisters" of Jesus mentioned in Scripture are reinterpreted as cousins or Joseph's children from a previous marriage.

### What Scripture Teaches

Scripture explicitly names Jesus's brothers:

> "Is this not the carpenter's son? Is not His mother called Mary? And His brothers **James, Joses, Simon, and Judas**? And His sisters, are they not all with us?" (Matthew 13:55-56)

> "Is this not the carpenter, the Son of Mary, and **brother of James, Joses, Judas, and Simon**? And are not His sisters here with us?" (Mark 6:3)

The Greek word used is **adelphos** — which means *brother* (biological sibling). It is a different word from *anepsios* (cousin). When the New Testament means cousin, it uses *anepsios* (Colossians 4:10). The four named individuals are consistently called *adelphoi* (brothers), not cousins.

Scripture implies that Joseph and Mary had a normal marital relationship after Jesus's birth:

> "Then Joseph... took to him his wife, and **did not know her till she had brought forth her firstborn Son**. And he called His name Jesus." (Matthew 1:24-25)

"Did not know her till" (*heos hou*) in Greek clearly implies that after the specified event (the birth), the implied restriction ended. Normal marital relations followed.

> "Is not this the carpenter's son?... and **his brothers** James and Joses and Simon and Judas?" (Matthew 13:55)

### Historical Note

The perpetual virginity doctrine was promoted by Jerome (~383 CE) against earlier views that accepted Jesus's brothers as literal siblings. It grew out of the early church's developing asceticism — the idea that virginity was spiritually superior to marriage (which Scripture explicitly calls "a doctrine of demons" — 1 Timothy 4:1-3).

**Conclusion**: The perpetual virginity of Mary is directly contradicted by Matthew 1:25 and the consistent use of *adelphos* (biological brother) in Matthew 13:55 and Mark 6:3.

## Marian Doctrine 4: The Assumption of Mary (Dogma, 1950 CE)

### What the Catholic Church Teaches

**Pope Pius XII, Munificentissimus Deus (1950 CE)**: "...the Immaculate Mother of God, the ever Virgin Mary, having completed the course of her earthly life, was assumed body and soul into heavenly glory."

This teaches that Mary was taken to heaven bodily — either without dying, or immediately after dying. It was defined as infallible dogma on November 1, 1950.

### What Scripture Teaches

**Scripture says absolutely nothing about the death or bodily assumption of Mary.** It is completely silent on the matter.

The doctrine rests entirely on tradition — specifically on apocryphal documents from the 4th–6th centuries CE, none of which are canonical Scripture.

**God's Word records two instances of individuals being taken to heaven:** 

> "And Enoch **walked with God; and he was not, for God took him**." (Genesis 5:24)

> "...and behold, a chariot of fire appeared with horses of fire... and **Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven.**" (2 Kings 2:11)

These are recorded in Scripture explicitly because they were exceptional. There is no comparable record for Mary.

**Peter on David's death and state** (Acts 2:29, 34) confirmed even great men of God remain in the grave until the resurrection:

> "Men and brethren, let me speak freely to you of the patriarch David, that he is both **dead and buried**, and his tomb is with us to this day... For David **did not ascend into the heavens**." (Acts 2:29, 34)

**Conclusion**: The Assumption of Mary is a doctrine with zero biblical support — invented from post-biblical tradition and defined as dogma in 1950 CE. If such an event had occurred, Scripture — which records far less significant events — would not be silent about it.

## Part 2: The Veneration of Mary — Is It Worship?

The Catholic Church distinguishes between *latria* (worship — given to God alone), *dulia* (veneration — given to saints), and *hyperdulia* (special veneration — given to Mary). The Church insists Mary receives *hyperdulia*, not *latria* (worship).

However, the practical acts performed in Marian devotion must be tested by Scripture:

| **Practice** | **Done Toward Mary** | **Scripture Verdict** |
|-------------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Bowing before her image | Yes | 2nd Commandment forbids bowing to images (Exodus 20:4-5) |
| Praying to her | Yes | Only God receives prayer (Matthew 6:9); only one mediator (1 Timothy 2:5) |
| Calling her "Holy Mother of God" | Yes | "Holy" applied to God alone (Isaiah 6:3; Revelation 4:8) |
| Lighting candles before her image | Yes | Part of image-veneration forbidden (Deuteronomy 4:15-19) |
| Crediting her with miracles and healings | Yes | Miracles attributed to God alone in Scripture |
| Seeking her intercession at death | Yes | Dead cannot intercede (Ecclesiastes 9:5; Deuteronomy 18:10-12) |

The distinction between *hyperdulia* and *latria* is a theological construct not found in Scripture. Scripture knows only one category for these acts: worship — and assigns them to God alone.

> "You shall worship the LORD your God, and Him only you shall serve." (Matthew 4:10, Jesus quoting Deuteronomy 6:13)

## Part 3: The Pagan Origins of Mary Veneration

Mary veneration in the form practiced by Catholicism shows striking parallels to widespread goddess worship in the ancient world:

- **Isis (Egypt)**: Queen of Heaven, mother of Horus (the divine child), depicted with a halo and child on her lap — virtually identical iconography to Catholic Madonna and Child images
- **Cybele (Rome)**: Mother Goddess of the gods, "Mother of the Gods," protectress
- **Diana/Artemis (Ephesus)**: "Great is Diana of the Ephesians" — the city where Mary devotion first became prominent
- **Queen of Heaven (Jeremiah)**: God rebuked Israel explicitly for worshipping a "Queen of Heaven":

> "The children gather wood, the fathers kindle the fire, and the women knead dough, to make cakes for the **queen of heaven**... to provoke Me to anger." (Jeremiah 7:18)

> "But we will certainly do whatever has gone out of our own mouth, to burn incense to the **queen of heaven** and pour out drink offerings to her..." (Jeremiah 44:17)

God's response:

> "...your burnt offerings are not acceptable, nor your sacrifices sweet to Me." (Jeremiah 6:20)

The title "Queen of Heaven" is applied to Mary in Catholic devotion — a title God explicitly condemned in Israel.

## Conclusion: Honour vs. Worship

Mary was chosen by God for an extraordinary role and deserves honor as a servant of the Most High. But she herself would reject the titles, prayers, and doctrines that have been built around her — because she, above all others, knew that her Son alone was the way, the truth, and the life.

Her own instruction remains the final word on what she would want:

> **"Whatever He says to you, do it."** (John 2:5)

Not: "Come to me." Not: "Pray through me." Not: "I am your co-redemptrix."

Simply: Go to Him. Do what He says.
