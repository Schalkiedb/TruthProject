# False Doctrine 14: Sacred Tradition vs. Scripture — Who Has Final Authority?
## A Bible-Only Study

## Introduction: The Root of Every Other False Doctrine

Every false doctrine examined in this series shares a common foundation: **something other than Scripture was placed above or alongside God's Word as a source of religious authority.** Whether it was Church tradition, council decisions, papal pronouncements, or Greek philosophy — the pattern is the same.

This study examines the most foundational question of all: **Does God's Word alone have final authority, or does human tradition — however ancient or sincerely held — have equal or greater authority?**

How you answer this question determines everything else.

> "To the law and to the testimony! If they do not speak according to this word, **it is because there is no light in them.**" (Isaiah 8:20)

## Part 1: The Catholic Teaching on Tradition

### 1.1 Council of Trent (1545–1563 CE) — The Formal Definition

The Council of Trent was convened specifically to respond to the Protestant Reformation's cry of *Sola Scriptura* (Scripture alone). Its decree on Scripture and Tradition states:

"The Council clearly perceives that this truth and discipline are contained in the written books and in the **unwritten traditions**... following the example of the orthodox Fathers, it receives and venerates with an equal affection of piety and reverence **all the books both of the Old and New Testaments** — since God alone is the author of both — together with the said **Traditions**, as well those appertaining to faith as to morals, as having been dictated, either by Christ's own word of mouth, or by the Holy Ghost, and **preserved in the Catholic Church by a continuous succession**."

Key phrase: **"equal affection of piety and reverence"** — Tradition is placed on equal footing with Scripture.

### 1.2 Vatican II (1965 CE) — Reaffirmed and Expanded

*Dei Verbum* (Dogmatic Constitution on Divine Revelation): "Sacred tradition and Sacred Scripture form one sacred deposit of the word of God, committed to the Church."

**Catechism of the Catholic Church (CCC 82-83)**: "Both Scripture and Tradition must be accepted and honored with equal sentiments of devotion and reverence."

### 1.3 Practical Consequence

When Scripture and Tradition conflict — **Tradition wins**, because the Church claims that Tradition is the authentic interpretation of Scripture, and only the Church (through the Magisterium) has authority to interpret it.

This is how:
- Infant baptism is justified (no Scripture; Tradition claims apostolic origin)
- Purgatory is justified (no Scripture; Tradition and council definition)
- Mary's Assumption is justified (no Scripture; papal declaration in 1950)
- Sunday worship is justified (no Scripture; Tradition of the Church)
- The Rosary is justified (no Scripture; medieval development)

The Church openly and proudly admits this logic. A widely quoted Catholic source states:

> "The Bible alone cannot prove Sunday worship. The authority of the Church could therefore use its own power to set aside the precept of the Mosaic Law, and substitute for it Sunday." (*Catholic Record*, London, Ontario, September 1, 1923)

In other words: **We changed it because we have authority to change it** — even against Scripture.

## Part 2: What Scripture Says About Its Own Sufficiency

### 2.1 The Complete Sufficiency of Scripture

> "All Scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is **profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness, that the man of God may be complete, thoroughly equipped for every good work.**" (2 Timothy 3:16-17)

**"Complete, thoroughly equipped for every good work"** — if Scripture makes the man of God *complete* and *thoroughly equipped*, what role is there for supplementary, extra-biblical tradition? The text is all-encompassing.

The Greek word translated "complete" (*artios*) means "fitted out, capable, proficient." The Greek for "thoroughly equipped" (*exartizō*) means "fully furnished." Scripture alone provides everything needed.

> "For the word of God is living and powerful, and **sharper than any two-edged sword**, piercing even to the division of soul and spirit, and of joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart." (Hebrews 4:12)

The Word of God is alive and sufficient — it discerns, it penetrates, it equips. No tradition can do what only God's Word can.

### 2.2 The Finality of God's Written Word

> "You shall **not add to the word** which I command you, nor take from it, that you may keep the commandments of the LORD your God which I command you." (Deuteronomy 4:2)

> "**Every word of God is pure**... **Do not add to His words**, lest He rebuke you, and you be found a liar." (Proverbs 30:5-6)

> "For I testify to everyone who hears the words of the prophecy of this book: **If anyone adds to these things, God will add to him the plagues** that are written in this book; and if anyone takes away from the words of the book of this prophecy, **God shall take away his part from the Book of Life**." (Revelation 22:18-19)

God's prohibition on addition to His Word is consistent from Deuteronomy to Revelation. Any system that places human tradition **alongside or above** Scripture is directly violating these commands.

## Part 3: Jesus vs. Pharisaic Tradition — The Same Pattern

The conflict between Scripture and human tradition is not new. Jesus addressed exactly this structure — the elevation of human tradition to the level of God's Word — in His confrontation with the Pharisees.

> "Then the Pharisees and scribes asked Him, 'Why do Your disciples not walk according to the tradition of the elders...?' He answered and said to them, '**Well did Isaiah prophesy of you hypocrites**... "This people honors Me with their lips, but their heart is far from Me. And **in vain they worship Me, teaching as doctrines the commandments of men**."'" (Mark 7:5-7)

**"In vain they worship Me"** — worship based on human tradition rather than God's commands is empty. It achieves nothing.

> "For **laying aside the commandment of God**, you hold the tradition of men." (Mark 7:8)

> "All too well you **reject the commandment of God**, that you may **keep your tradition**." (Mark 7:9)

> "...making the **word of God of no effect through your tradition**." (Mark 7:13)

The same dynamic that Jesus condemned among the Pharisees — elevating human tradition to the level of God's commanded Word — is precisely what the Council of Trent formalized.

## Part 4: The Apostolic Standard — Scripture as the Test

> "These were more fair-minded than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness, and **searched the Scriptures daily to find out whether these things were so**." (Acts 17:11)

The Bereans tested even the apostle Paul's teaching against Scripture. Paul **commended** them for it. This is the apostolic norm: Scripture tests everything — including tradition and apostolic claims.

> "But even if we, or an **angel from heaven**, preach any other gospel to you than what we have preached to you, **let him be accursed**." (Galatians 1:8)

Paul places the apostles AND angels BELOW the standard of the gospel as delivered. The test is the Word — not the teacher, however exalted.

> "Do not add to or take away from [the things I am commanding you]." (Deuteronomy 12:32)

> "For I have not shunned to declare to you **the whole counsel of God**." (Acts 20:27)

## Part 5: Apostolic Succession — The Authority Claim Behind Tradition

The doctrine of Sacred Tradition leans on **Apostolic Succession** — the claim that authority flows through an unbroken chain of ordained bishops from the apostles to the present Pope, guaranteeing authentic interpretation of Scripture and Tradition.

### 5.1 What Scripture Says About Authority

**Authority was given to the WORD, not to a succession of bishops**:

> "Heaven and earth will pass away, but **My words will by no means pass away.**" (Matthew 24:35)

> "The grass withers, the flower fades, but **the word of our God stands forever.**" (Isaiah 40:8)

> "Sanctify them by Your truth. **Your word is truth.**" (John 17:17)

God's Word is permanent, true, and authoritative — independent of any institutional succession.

### 5.2 Scripture's Own Test for a Prophet/Teacher

> "If there arises among you a prophet... and he says, 'Let us go after other gods'... you shall not listen to the words of that prophet... for the LORD your God is testing you to know whether you love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul." (Deuteronomy 13:1-3)

Even a miracle-working prophet is disqualified if he leads away from God's commandments. Position, succession, or signs do not validate a teacher. **Agreement with God's Word** validates a teacher.

### 5.3 The Historical Reality of Apostolic Succession

Even granting the Catholic claim, the "succession" is historically disputed: there are breaks in the documented chain, periods of multiple simultaneous popes (the "Great Schism"), popes condemned by later popes, and a documented period where the papacy was sold to the highest bidder (*simony*). The chain of succession required to be unbroken has been repeatedly broken.

## Part 6: The Protestant Reformation — *Sola Scriptura*

The Reformers' slogan was **four "Solas"** (Latin: "alone"):
- *Sola Scriptura* — Scripture alone (authority)
- *Sola Gratia* — Grace alone (salvation)
- *Sola Fide* — Faith alone (justification)
- *Solus Christus* — Christ alone (mediator)
- *Soli Deo Gloria* — Glory to God alone

**Luther's statement at the Diet of Worms (1521 CE)** — when asked to recant:

> "Unless I am convinced by the testimony of the Holy Scriptures or by evident reason... **My conscience is captive to the Word of God, and I neither can nor will recant anything**, since it is neither right nor safe to act against conscience. I can do no other."

Luther refused to place tradition or council decisions above Scripture. This was revolutionary because it re-established the authority that had always been Scripture's rightful place.

## Conclusion: The First and Most Important Question

Every other reform, every other doctrinal correction, every other step back toward biblical truth — depends on first answering this question correctly: **Who has the final word?**

If Church tradition shares authority with Scripture, then:
- The Rosary can be defended
- Infant baptism can be justified
- The Sabbath change can be authorized
- Mary can be made co-redemptrix
- Purgatory can be taught

But if Scripture alone is the final authority — as Jesus treated it (Matthew 4:4, 7, 10 — "It is written"), as Paul declared it (2 Timothy 3:16-17), as the Bereans practiced it (Acts 17:11) — then every doctrine must be measured against God's Word and judged accordingly.

> **"If they do not speak according to this word, it is because there is no light in them."** (Isaiah 8:20)

The standard is established. The test is given. The Word stands forever.
