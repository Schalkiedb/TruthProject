# False Doctrine 13: Infant Baptism and Sprinkling — What Is Biblical Baptism?
## A Bible-Only Study

---

## Introduction: Two Questions, One Standard

Biblical baptism involves two questions that must be answered from Scripture:

1. **Who is the proper candidate?** (Adult believer — or infant?)
2. **What is the proper mode?** (Full immersion — or sprinkling?)

Both answers flow directly from what baptism is and what it symbolizes. The Bible is consistent and clear on both points. The alternative practices entered Christianity from outside the apostolic tradition and were not universally adopted until several centuries after the apostles.

> "One Lord, one faith, **one baptism**." (Ephesians 4:5)

There is ONE baptism — and Scripture defines it precisely.

---

## Part 1: The Biblical Order — Believe FIRST, Baptize SECOND

In every single baptism account in the New Testament, the sequence is identical:

**First: Hear and believe. Then: Be baptized.**

> "He who **believes** and is baptized will be saved; but he who does not believe will be condemned." (Mark 16:16)

**Belief precedes baptism** in this sequence. Note: the condemnation is for unbelief — not for not being baptized. Baptism is the expression of pre-existing faith, not the cause of salvation.

> "Then Peter said to them, '**Repent**, and let every one of you be baptized...'" (Acts 2:38)

**Repentance first** — which requires understanding, conviction, and willful turning — then baptism.

> "But when they **believed** Philip as he preached the things concerning the kingdom of God and the name of Jesus Christ, both men and women **were baptized**." (Acts 8:12)

> "Now as they went down the road, they came to some water. And the eunuch said, 'See, here is water. What hinders me from being baptized?' Then Philip said, '**If you believe with all your heart, you may.**' And he answered and said, '**I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God.**'" (Acts 8:36-37)

The requirement stated explicitly: **believing with all your heart**. Philip does not baptize until the eunuch makes a confession of faith.

> "Then Crispus, the ruler of the synagogue, **believed on the Lord** with all his household, and many of the Corinthians, **hearing, believed and were baptized.**" (Acts 18:8)

The pattern: **Heard → Believed → Baptized.** Always in this order.

---

## Part 2: Infants Cannot Meet the Biblical Requirements

The biblical requirements for baptism — in every account in the New Testament — are:

| **Requirement** | **Scripture** | **Can an Infant Do This?** |
|----------------|--------------|--------------------------|
| Repentance | Acts 2:38 | No |
| Belief with the whole heart | Acts 8:37 | No |
| Hearing and believing the gospel | Acts 18:8 | No |
| Confessing Jesus as Lord | Romans 10:9-10 | No |
| Understanding and willful choice | Acts 2:41 ("those who gladly received his word") | No |

An infant cannot repent, believe, confess, understand, or choose. Therefore an infant cannot be a candidate for biblical baptism.

---

## Part 3: The Origin of Infant Baptism

**Infant baptism was not the practice of the apostolic church.** It was gradually introduced:

- **Origen (~185–254 CE)**: First explicit mention — Origen noted that the church practiced infant baptism and claimed it came from the apostles (a claim with no documented support)
- **Tertullian (~200 CE)**: Actually **discouraged** infant baptism, suggesting it was better for children to wait until they could understand and commit
- **Augustine (~354–430 CE)**: The crucial turning point. Augustine developed and popularized the doctrine of *original guilt* — that infants are guilty and under condemnation from birth due to Adam's sin — which made infant baptism seem urgent (to wash away guilt)
- **Council of Carthage (418 CE)**: Condemned those who denied infant baptism
- **Council of Trent (1545–1563 CE)**: Formally canonized infant baptism as dogma

The theological driver was not apostolic teaching but Augustine's doctrine of original guilt requiring baptismal washing for infants to avoid damnation — a doctrine that itself lacks clear biblical support.

---

## Part 4: What About Household Baptisms?

The Catholic defense most often cites "household baptisms" in Acts — arguing that households would have included infants:

- Cornelius's household: Acts 10:44-48
- Lydia's household: Acts 16:14-15
- The Philippian jailer's household: Acts 16:31-34
- Crispus's household: Acts 18:8
- Stephanas's household: 1 Corinthians 1:16

### Examining Each Case

**Acts 10 — Cornelius's Household**: "The Holy Spirit fell upon all those who heard the word" (v.44). They received the Holy Spirit before baptism — a conscious experience of the Spirit. Infants cannot experience this.

**Acts 16:31-34 — The Philippian Jailer**: "Then he... was baptized, he and all his family. And... he rejoiced, having believed in God with all his household." All of them rejoiced. All of them **believed**. Rejoicing and believing require conscious comprehension.

**Acts 18:8 — Crispus**: "All his household... believed and were baptized." Explicit: all believed.

**1 Corinthians 1:16 — Stephanas**: Paul elsewhere describes the household of Stephanas as those who "devoted themselves to the ministry of the saints" (1 Corinthians 16:15) — active, conscious service.

In none of these accounts is there any indication that infants were present. All the descriptions assume conscious participants who heard, believed, and rejoiced.

## Part 5: The Mode — Immersion, Not Sprinkling

### 5.1 The Greek Word *Baptizo* — "To Immerse/Dip"

The Greek word throughout the New Testament is **baptizo** (*βαπτίζω*), which means:
- To immerse
- To dip
- To plunge
- To submerge

It **never** means to sprinkle or pour in classical Greek usage. Sprinkling is *rhantizo* (a completely different Greek word, used in Hebrews 9:13-19 for the Old Testament ritual sprinkling of blood — a word never used for baptism).

When the translators of the King James Bible (1611) encountered *baptizo*, they chose to transliterate it (write it in English letters) rather than translate it — because if they had translated it literally, "immerse," it would have condemned the Church of England's practice of infant sprinkling.

### 5.2 Jesus's Baptism — The Pattern

> "When He had been baptized, Jesus came up **immediately from the water**." (Matthew 3:16)

Coming **up from** the water presupposes being put into it. You cannot come up from water you were merely sprinkled with.

> "And John also was baptizing in Aenon near Salim, because **there was much water there**." (John 3:23)

John chose this location specifically because "there was much water there." For sprinkling or pouring you need a cup of water, not a river or large body. The reason for "much water" was immersion.

### 5.3 Paul's Theology of Baptism — The Mode Is the Meaning

> "Or do you not know that as many of us as were **baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death? Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death**, that just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life." (Romans 6:3-4)

**The symbolism requires the mode**:
- Going under the water = **burial** with Christ (His death)
- Coming up from the water = **resurrection** with Christ (new life)

Sprinkling cannot symbolize burial and resurrection. Immersion perfectly symbolizes burial and resurrection. The mode is not arbitrary — it is the whole point of the ordinance.

> "...buried with Him in baptism, in which you also were raised with Him through faith in the working of God, who raised Him from the dead." (Colossians 2:12)

---

## Part 6: When Did Sprinkling Enter?

**284–305 CE**: The *Didache* (~100 CE) already mentions sprinkling as an emergency option when "you have no running water" — but specifically for exceptional circumstances only (illness, danger of death), not as standard practice. Running water (immersion) is the stated norm.

**13th century CE**: Sprinkling (*aspersion*) became more common in Western Europe — practically, due to cold climates and large numbers being baptized at once. The theological justification came after the practical convenience.

**Council of Trent (1545–1563 CE)**: Affirmed sprinkling and pouring as equally valid with immersion — over Protestant objection.

## Part 7: Summary Comparison Table

| **Topic** | **Catholic / Many Protestant Traditions** | **Biblical Standard** |
|-----------|-------------------------------------------|-----------------------|
| Who is baptized | Infants and believers | Believers only — repent first, then baptize (Acts 2:38; Mark 16:16) |
| Mode | Sprinkling or pouring | Immersion — *baptizo* means to immerse; Romans 6:3-4 requires full burial symbolism |
| What baptism represents | Washing away of original sin | Death (burial) and resurrection with Christ (Romans 6:3-4; Colossians 2:12) |
| Prerequisite | Sponsorship by godparents (infants) | Personal belief, repentance, and confession (Acts 8:37; Acts 2:38) |
| Effect | Regeneration / removal of original guilt | Public declaration of pre-existing faith and commitment |
| Historical origin | Augustine's original guilt doctrine (4th century CE); Council of Carthage (418 CE) | Apostolic practice: adult believers immersed upon confession (Acts 2, 8, 16, 18) |
| Greek word | *Baptizo* = sprinkle (reinterpreted) | *Baptizo* = to immerse/dip (classical Greek; context of NT confirms) |

---

## Conclusion

Biblical baptism is:
1. **For believers** — those who have heard, understood, repented, and believed
2. **By immersion** — because *baptizo* means immersion, and because the burial/resurrection symbolism requires it
3. **A public declaration** — an outward sign of an inward reality that already exists (faith and regeneration), not the cause of it

Infant baptism replaces faith with a parental decision. Sprinkling replaces meaningful symbolism with a convenient substitute. Both represent departures from the clear apostolic practice documented throughout the Book of Acts.

The invitation remains open and clear:

> "Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit." (Acts 2:38)

Hear. Believe. Repent. Be baptized. In that order.
