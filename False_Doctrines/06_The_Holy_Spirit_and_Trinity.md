# False Doctrine 6: The Trinity Doctrine — What Does Scripture Actually Teach?
## A Bible-Only Study

---

## Introduction: The Need for Careful Study

Few doctrines are considered more foundational to mainstream Christianity than the Trinity — the belief that God is one Being existing in three co-equal, co-eternal, co-identical persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

This study is not written to dismiss the greatness of the Father, the reality of Jesus Christ, or the reality of the Holy Spirit. All three are fully affirmed throughout this study. The question is: **Does the specific formulation of the Trinity doctrine — as defined at Nicaea (325 CE) and Constantinople (381 CE) — actually reflect what Scripture teaches?**

The answer requires a careful, honest engagement with the Bible's actual language.

> "To the law and to the testimony! If they do not speak according to this word, it is because there is no light in them." (Isaiah 8:20)

---

## Part 1: The Historical Development of the Trinity Doctrine

The Trinitarian formula was NOT the universal, uncontested belief of the early church. It developed through decades of controversy and was defined by councils deeply influenced by political pressure.

**Key Timeline**:
- **1st–2nd century CE**: Early church writings describe God as the Father, Jesus as His Son, and the Holy Spirit as God's Spirit/power — without the specific "three co-equal persons of one substance" language
- **325 CE — Council of Nicaea**: Emperor Constantine convened this council primarily to resolve the Arian controversy. The term *homoousios* ("same substance") was adopted to define the relationship of the Son to the Father — though this specific word does not appear in Scripture. Constantine himself presided, though unbaptized
- **381 CE — Council of Constantinople**: Holy Spirit formally included as the third person of the Trinity, "co-equal and co-eternal" with Father and Son
- **589 CE — Third Council of Toledo**: Added the *filioque* clause to the Nicene Creed — "the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father AND the Son" — the change that ultimately split Eastern and Western Christianity

**The word "Trinity" (Latin: *Trinitas*)** does not appear in Scripture. It was coined by Tertullian (~200 CE) and systematized over the following centuries.

## Part 2: Who Is God the Father? — The Biblical Answer

### 2.1 The Shema — Israel's Central Confession

> "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is **one**." (Deuteronomy 6:4)

Jesus Himself affirmed this as the first and greatest commandment:

> "Jesus answered him, '**The first of all the commandments is: "Hear, O Israel, the LORD our God, the LORD is one."**'" (Mark 12:29)

### 2.2 One God = The Father

> "Yet for us there is **one God, the Father**, from whom are all things and for whom we exist, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things and through whom we exist." (1 Corinthians 8:6, ESV)

Paul explicitly identifies the "one God" as **the Father** — a distinct person from "one Lord, Jesus Christ." These are not interchangeable.

> "There is **one God and Father of all**, who is above all, and through all, and in you all." (Ephesians 4:6)

> "...the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom You have sent." (John 17:3 — Jesus praying to the Father)

Jesus distinguishes between Himself and "the only true God" who is His Father. He is not calling Himself "the only true God" here — He is addressing His Father by that title.

## Part 3: Who Is Jesus Christ? — The Biblical Answer

Scripture consistently presents Jesus as:
1. Truly divine — sharing the Father's nature
2. Truly distinct — a separate person from the Father
3. Subordinate in role — the Son sent by the Father

### 3.1 Jesus Is Genuinely Divine

> "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and **the Word was God**. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through Him, and without Him nothing was made that was made." (John 1:1-3)

> "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the **only begotten of the Father**, full of grace and truth." (John 1:14)

> "For in Him dwells all the **fullness of the Godhead bodily**." (Colossians 2:9)

### 3.2 Jesus Is Distinct from the Father

> "And the Word was **with** God." (John 1:1) — "with" (Greek *pros*) implies face-to-face distinction

> "Who, being in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal with God, but made Himself of no reputation, taking the form of a bondservant, and **coming in the likeness of men**." (Philippians 2:6-7)

> "I came forth from the **Father** and have come into the world. Again, I leave the world and go to the **Father**." (John 16:28)

> "For God so loved the world that He gave **His only begotten Son**..." (John 3:16)

A Son must have a Father who is distinct from him.

### 3.3 Jesus Is Subordinate in Role to the Father

These passages are not diminishments of Christ's nature — they reflect the relationship between Father and Son:

> "**The Father is greater than I.**" (John 14:28)

> "But about that day or hour no one knows, not even the angels in heaven, **nor the Son, but only the Father.**" (Matthew 24:36; Mark 13:32)

> "I can of **Myself do nothing**... because I do not seek My own will but the will of **the Father who sent Me.**" (John 5:30)

> "Now when all things are made subject to Him, then the **Son Himself will also be subject to Him** who put all things under Him, that God may be all in all." (1 Corinthians 15:28)

**A Trinity of three co-equal persons creates interpretive problems for all of these verses.** If the Son is fully co-equal with the Father in every sense, how can the Father be "greater," the Son not know what the Father knows, and the Son ultimately be "subject to" the Father?

## Part 4: Who Is the Holy Spirit? — The Biblical Answer

### 4.1 The Holy Spirit Is the Shared Presence and Power of the Father and Son

> "But you are not in the flesh but in the Spirit, if indeed the **Spirit of God** dwells in you. Now if anyone does not have the **Spirit of Christ**, he is not His." (Romans 8:9)

**The Spirit of God** and **the Spirit of Christ** are used interchangeably in a single sentence. The Holy Spirit is the presence of both the Father and the Son — not a separate third being.

> "I will not leave you orphans; **I will come to you.**" (John 14:18)

Jesus said HE would come to them — then immediately discusses the coming of the Holy Spirit (John 14:16). The coming of the Holy Spirit IS Christ's spiritual presence.

> "...that Christ may **dwell in your hearts** through faith..." (Ephesians 3:17)

Christ dwells in hearts through the Holy Spirit — this is God's presence with believers.

> "**Christ in you**, the hope of glory." (Colossians 1:27)

> "I [Paul] have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but **Christ lives in me**." (Galatians 2:20)

### 4.2 The Holy Spirit Is Described as the Father's Spirit

> "For it is not you who speak, but the **Spirit of your Father** who speaks in you." (Matthew 10:20)

> "And when they had prayed, the place where they were assembled together was shaken; and they were all filled with the **Holy Spirit**, and they spoke the word of God with boldness." (Acts 4:31) [= the Father's Spirit poured out — Acts 2:17-18 equates with Joel 2:28]

### 4.3 The Spirit Speaks What It "Hears" — From the Father and Son

> "However, when He, the Spirit of truth, has come, He will guide you into all truth; for **He will not speak on His own authority, but whatever He hears He will speak**; and He will tell you things to come. He will glorify Me, for **He will take of what is Mine and declare it to you**. All things that the Father has are Mine. Therefore I said that **He will take of Mine and declare it to you.**" (John 16:13-15)

If the Holy Spirit were a fully co-equal, co-eternal separate person, speaking "on His own authority" — with His own independent will — would not be a restriction. The Spirit speaks what He "hears" from the Father and Son, and takes what belongs to THEM to declare. This is the shared presence and communication of the Father and Son—not an independent third being.

## Part 5: The Apostolic Greetings — A Decisive Pattern

If the Holy Spirit were a third co-equal person of the Godhead, we would expect the apostles to include Him in their greetings alongside the Father and Son. They never do.

> "Grace to you and peace from **God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ**." (Romans 1:7; 1 Corinthians 1:3; 2 Corinthians 1:2; Galatians 1:3; Ephesians 1:2; Philippians 1:2; Colossians 1:2; 1 Thessalonians 1:1; 2 Thessalonians 1:2; Philemon 1:3)

This greeting pattern appears in TWELVE Pauline letters — always "God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ." **The Holy Spirit is never listed as a separate sender.**

> "Grace and peace from **God the Father and from our Lord Jesus Christ**, the Son of the Father..." (2 John 1:3)

> "From **Him who is and who was and who is to come** [the Father], and from the **seven Spirits** who are before His throne, and from **Jesus Christ**, the faithful witness..." (Revelation 1:4-5)

## Part 6: The Textual Problems — Verses Used to Prove The Trinity

### 6.1 The Johannine Comma — 1 John 5:7 (KJV)

> "For there are **three that bear record in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one**." (1 John 5:7, KJV)

This is the most explicit Trinitarian statement in the New Testament — and it is **a proven forgery, not in the original Greek**.

**The facts**:
- This verse is **absent from ALL Greek manuscripts except 4 late, minor ones** (all post-14th century)
- No Greek church father ever quoted it to defend the Trinity in any debate — though they desperately needed it during fierce Trinitarian controversies
- It first appears in a **Latin translation** in the 8th century CE
- **Erasmus** (who compiled the Greek New Testament in 1516 CE) was pressured to include it after he initially excluded it due to lack of manuscript evidence
- **ALL major modern translations** (NIV, ESV, NASB, RSV, NRSV, NLT, CSB) omit it or footnote it as a later addition
- Biblical scholars of all theological backgrounds agree it is the "Johannine Comma" — a scribal interpolation

**This verse was inserted into Scripture to support the Trinity doctrine. It is not original.**

---

### 6.2 Matthew 28:19 — "In the Name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit"

> "Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them **in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit**." (Matthew 28:19)

This is genuine — but observe several important points:

**1. The word is "name" singular, not "names" plural** — though three are mentioned, they share A SINGLE name. This is consistent with the Father and Son having one divine name, and the Spirit being their shared presence — not three separate beings with separate names.

**2. Throughout Acts, baptism is consistently performed "in the name of Jesus Christ" (singular)**:
- Acts 2:38 — "be baptized... **in the name of Jesus Christ**"
- Acts 8:16 — "baptized **in the name of the Lord Jesus**"
- Acts 10:48 — "baptized **in the name of the Lord**"
- Acts 19:5 — "baptized **in the name of the Lord Jesus**"
- Acts 22:16 — "be baptized, and wash away your sins, **calling on the name of the Lord**"

**The apostles never baptized using the Trinitarian formula.** They baptized **in Jesus's name**.

**3. Eusebius of Caesarea** (260–340 CE), the leading church historian who was PRESENT at the Council of Nicaea, quoted Matthew 28:19 in EIGHTEEN of his writings BEFORE Nicaea — and consistently wrote "in **My name**" instead of "in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit." After Nicaea, his quotations changed. This raises serious questions about potential textual alteration.

---

### 6.3 "Godhead" in the KJV

> "For **the invisible things of him** from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and **Godhead** (*theiotēs*)." (Romans 1:20, KJV)

> "For in him dwelleth all the fulness of the **Godhead** (*theotēs*) bodily." (Colossians 2:9, KJV)

The Greek words rendered "Godhead" are **theiotes** (Romans 1:20) and **theotes** (Colossians 2:9), meaning "divine nature" or "deity" — NOT "three persons in one substance." The word "Godhead" as translated in the KJV does not inherently carry a Trinitarian meaning. Modern translations render these as "divine nature" (ESV, NIV) or "deity" (NASB).

## Part 7: Jesus as Michael the Archangel — A Biblical Case

This identification is rarely discussed openly but has solid scriptural support and helps explain the relationship between the Father and Son.

**"Michael" means "Who is like God?"** — a fitting name for the Son who bears God's image and nature.

**"Archangel" (*archaggelou*)** = Chief Commander/Chief Messenger — the highest angelic authority, who is distinct from created angels.

### Biblical Evidence

**1. Daniel — "One like the Son of God"**:

> [In the fiery furnace] "Look! I see four men loose, walking in the midst of the fire... and **the form of the fourth is like the Son of God.**" (Daniel 3:25)

> King Nebuchadnezzar: "Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-Nego, who sent **His Angel** and delivered His servants..." (Daniel 3:28)

The Bible uses "Son of God" and "His Angel" interchangeably for the same individual. This is consistent with Christ appearing as the Angel of the LORD throughout the Old Testament.

**2. Voice at the Resurrection**:

> "For the Lord Himself will descend from heaven with a shout, with **the voice of an archangel**, and with the trumpet of God. And **the dead in Christ will rise first.**" (1 Thessalonians 4:16)

> "...the hour is coming in which all who are in the graves will hear **His [Christ's] voice** and come forth." (John 5:28-29)

The SAME voice that raises the dead in John 5 is called "the voice of an archangel" in 1 Thessalonians 4:16. = Christ's voice is the archangel's voice.

**3. Michael Contends for God's People**:

> "Yet Michael the archangel, when contending with the devil, he disputed about the body of Moses..." (Jude 9)

> "And war broke out in heaven: **Michael and his angels fought with the dragon**; and the dragon and his angels fought, but they did not prevail, nor was a place found for them in heaven any longer. So the great dragon was cast out... **Revelation 12:7-9**"

Both Jude and Revelation show Michael as the primary divine champion against Satan — consistent with Christ's role throughout Scripture.

**4. Angels Refuse Worship — But Jesus Accepts It**:

> "Then I fell at his [angel's] feet to worship him. But he said to me, '**See that you do not do that!** I am your fellow servant... Worship God!'" (Revelation 19:10; also 22:8-9)

Created angels categorically refuse worship.

> "And when they saw Him [Jesus], **they worshiped Him.**" (Matthew 28:17)
> "Let all God's **angels worship Him [the Son].**" (Hebrews 1:6)

Jesus — identified with Michael — **receives and accepts worship** from both humans and angels. A created being cannot receive worship. Therefore "Michael" is not a created being but the pre-existent Son of God in His role as Chief Commander.

## Conclusion: What Scripture Actually Teaches

Scripture teaches:
1. **One God = the Father** — the ultimate source and authority (1 Corinthians 8:6; Deuteronomy 6:4; John 17:3)
2. **Jesus Christ = the only begotten Son** — fully divine, sharing the Father's nature, distinct in person, subordinate in role (John 1:1-3; John 16:28; Philippians 2:6-7; John 14:28)
3. **The Holy Spirit = the Father and Son's shared presence and power** — not an independently existing third person, but the way the Father and Son dwell in and communicate with believers (Romans 8:9; John 14:16-18; Colossians 1:27)

This does NOT reduce Christ or dismiss the Holy Spirit. It accurately reflects the language Scripture actually uses, rather than the metaphysical formulas developed by post-biblical councils under political pressure.

The unity of God is not the unity of "three persons in one substance" (a concept Paul and Jesus never articulated). It is the unity of Father and Son — like the unity of marriage (Genesis 2:24 "they shall become **one flesh**" — two persons, truly one).

> **"Now this is eternal life: that they know You, the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom You have sent."** (John 17:3)

The path to eternal life runs through knowing the Father as the "only true God" and Jesus as His sent Son — exactly as Jesus Himself defined it.
