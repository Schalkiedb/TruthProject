# False Doctrine 3: Hell — Eternal Torment or Final Destruction?
## A Bible-Only Study

---

## Introduction: Why the Traditional View Must Be Examined

The traditional doctrine of hell—eternal, conscious torment of the wicked in fire that never ends—is perhaps the doctrine most people accept without investigating what Scripture actually says. It shapes how billions of people view God's character.

This study does not begin with sentiment. It begins with Scripture, working through the original language, examining every relevant passage, and asking the text to speak for itself.

The question is not: "Is hell real?" It is: **"What does God's Word say hell actually is?"**

> "And if anyone takes away from the words of the book of this prophecy, God shall take away his part from the Book of Life, from the holy city, and from the things which are written in this book." (Revelation 22:19)

The same caution applies to adding concepts to Scripture that are not there.

---

## Part 1: Why There Is Confusion — Four Different Words Translated "Hell"

The word "hell" in English Bibles translates FOUR entirely different words from the original languages, each with a distinct meaning. Conflating them is the primary source of confusion.

---

### Word 1: SHEOL (Hebrew — Old Testament)

**Occurrences**: 65 times in the Old Testament
**Translation in various Bibles**: "hell," "grave," "pit," "death"
**Meaning**: The place of the dead — the grave. It applies to ALL the dead, righteous and wicked alike.

**Evidence that Sheol = the grave (not a fiery underworld)**:

> "For You will not leave **my soul in Sheol**, nor will You allow Your Holy One to see corruption." (Psalm 16:10 — quoted in Acts 2:27, applied to Christ's resurrection from the grave)

> "The wicked shall be turned into **Sheol**, and all the nations that forget God." (Psalm 9:17)

> "Jacob said... 'I shall go down into **Sheol** to my son in mourning.'" (Genesis 37:35 — Jacob expected to join his dead son in the grave)

> "If I ascend into heaven, You are there; if I make my bed in **Sheol**, You are there." (Psalm 139:8)

**Conclusion**: Sheol simply means the state of death—the grave. It is not a fiery torture chamber. Righteous people go to Sheol (Jacob, David). It is the domain of all who die.

---

### Word 2: HADES (Greek — New Testament)

**Occurrences**: 10 times in the New Testament
**Meaning**: The Greek equivalent of Sheol — the realm of the dead, the grave.

> "And in **Hades** he lifted up his eyes, being in torments, and saw Abraham afar off, and Lazarus in his bosom." (Luke 16:23) — [note: examined below]

> "For You will not leave **my soul in Hades**, nor will You allow Your Holy One to see corruption." (Acts 2:27 — quoting Psalm 16:10, applied to Jesus's resurrection from the grave)

> "Death and **Hades** delivered up the dead who were in them." (Revelation 20:13)

> "Then Death and **Hades** were cast into the lake of fire. This is the **second death**." (Revelation 20:14)

**Critical observation**: At the end, Hades itself is destroyed—thrown into the lake of fire. Hades is therefore NOT the final destination. It is a temporary state (the grave) that ends at the resurrection and final judgment.

---

### Word 3: GEHENNA (Greek — New Testament)

**Occurrences**: 12 times in the New Testament (11 from Jesus's own lips)
**Original meaning**: The Valley of Hinnom (*Gai-Hinnom → Gehenna*) — a physical valley outside Jerusalem.

**Historical background**: This valley was the garbage dump of Jerusalem. Fires burned continuously to consume refuse. It had also been the site where ancient Israelites burned their children to Molech (Jeremiah 7:31-32). Jeremiah prophesied it would become a valley of slaughter. By Jesus's day it was a symbol of total, consuming destruction.

Jesus used it as a metaphor for the final destruction of the wicked—complete, total annihilation.

> "And do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul. But rather fear **Him who is able to destroy both soul and body in Gehenna.**" (Matthew 10:28)

Note: "Destroy both soul AND body" — not "torment" but "destroy." The soul is not immortal; it can be destroyed.

> "And if your hand causes you to sin, cut it off. It is better for you to enter into life maimed, rather than having two hands, to go to **Gehenna**, into the fire that shall never be quenched." (Mark 9:43)

"Fire that shall never be quenched" means the fire is **unstoppable** — not that it burns forever. Even today, wildfires that cannot be controlled are called "unquenchable"—yet they eventually go out. The fire of Gehenna consumes completely.

**Malachi's supporting description**:

> "For behold, the day is coming, burning like an oven, and all the proud, yes, all who do wickedly will be stubble. And the day which is coming shall **burn them up**... They shall be **ashes under the soles of your feet** on the day that I do this." (Malachi 4:1, 3)

**Ashes** — not living people in eternal agony, but ashes. Total destruction.

---

### Word 4: TARTARUS (Greek — New Testament)

**Occurrences**: 1 time only

> "For if God did not spare the angels who sinned, but cast them down to **Tartarus** and delivered them into chains of darkness, to be reserved for judgment." (2 Peter 2:4)

**Meaning**: A Greek term for a place of confinement for fallen angels/demons — reserved until final judgment. This applies only to demonic beings, not to dead humans.

## Part 2: The Bible's Teaching on Final Punishment

### 2.1 The Wages of Sin

> "For **the wages of sin is death**, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord." (Romans 6:23)

The punishment for sin is clearly stated: **death** — not eternal torment in death. If eternal torment were the punishment, this verse would say "the wages of sin is eternal conscious suffering." It says death.

### 2.2 God Will Destroy, Not Endlessly Torment

> "For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have everlasting life." (John 3:16)

The alternative to everlasting life is **perishing** — not everlasting suffering. "Perish" (*apollumi* in Greek) means to be destroyed, to cease to exist.

> "Do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul. But rather fear **Him who is able to destroy both soul and body** in Gehenna." (Matthew 10:28)

God has the power to destroy the soul. A soul capable of being destroyed is NOT inherently immortal.

### 2.3 The Fire Consumes Completely

> "For behold, the day is coming, burning like an oven, and all the proud, yes, all who do wickedly will be **stubble**. And the day which is coming **shall burn them up**, says the LORD of hosts, 'That will leave them neither root nor branch.'" (Malachi 4:1)

> "But the wicked shall **perish**; and the enemies of the LORD, like the splendor of the meadows, shall **vanish. Into smoke they shall vanish away.**" (Psalm 37:20)

> "For yet a little while and **the wicked shall be no more**; indeed, you will look carefully for his place, but it shall be not." (Psalm 37:10)

## Part 3: The "Second Death" — What Is It?

> "And Death and Hades were cast into the lake of fire. This is the **second death**. And anyone not found written in the Book of Life was cast into the lake of fire." (Revelation 20:14-15)

> "But the cowardly, unbelieving, abominable, murderers, sexually immoral, sorcerers, idolaters, and all liars shall have their part in the lake which burns with fire and brimstone, which is the **second death**." (Revelation 21:8)

The lake of fire is explicitly defined as "the second death" — not "the second life in agony." Death means non-existence, cessation, destruction. The second death is the permanent, irreversible form of the first death.

> "He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the churches. He who overcomes shall not be hurt by the **second death.**" (Revelation 2:11)

The overcomer's reward is that they will NOT experience the second death — meaning the wicked WILL experience it as permanent destruction.

## Part 4: "Eternal" Fire — Does It Burn Forever?

**The word "eternal" (*aionios* in Greek)** refers to the *age-long* effect, not necessarily an endless ongoing process. The RESULT is eternal (permanent, irreversible), but the PROCESS need not be unending.

**Biblical proof** — Sodom and Gomorrah:

> "...as Sodom and Gomorrah, and the cities around them in a similar manner to these, having given themselves over to sexual immorality and gone after strange flesh, are set forth as an example, **suffering the vengeance of eternal fire.**" (Jude 7)

Sodom was destroyed by "eternal fire." Yet Sodom is not burning today. The fire was eternal in its *effect* — the cities were permanently, irreversibly destroyed. They still do not exist.

**The same logic applies to Gehenna's fire.** The fire is eternal in its result (total, irrevocable destruction), not a never-ending process of conscious torment.

## Part 5: "The Worm Does Not Die" — Examined

> "...where their worm does not die and the fire is not quenched." (Mark 9:48, quoting Isaiah 66:24)

Jesus quotes Isaiah. Read the original context:

> "And they shall go forth and look upon the **corpses** of the men who have transgressed against Me. For their worm shall not die, nor their fire be quenched; they shall be an abhorrence to all flesh." (Isaiah 66:24, emphasis added)

The word is **corpses** — dead bodies, not living people. The worms (maggots) consuming the bodies and the fire consuming refuse do not die or go out until the consumption is complete. This is a description of thorough destruction, not living people being tortured.

## Part 6: Rich Man and Lazarus (Luke 16:19-31) — Parable or Literal?

This is the most cited passage in support of conscious torment after death. Before applying it literally, consider:

**1. Every indicator marks this as a parable:**
- Located in a series of parables (Luke 15-16)
- Contains obviously symbolic elements: a literal conversation across an infinite gulf, Abraham's "bosom" as a geographical location, a soul in flames that can talk
- Jesus frequently used fictional characters to teach moral lessons

**2. Parables are not designed to teach doctrine about the afterlife:**
A parable's purpose is to illustrate a single principle. Jesus used this parable to warn rich people who ignore the poor and who would not believe "even if one rose from the dead" (prophetically pointing to His own resurrection being rejected).

**3. The passage contradicts the literal rest of Scripture if taken literally:**
- The dead are in Hades (the grave) — not heaven, not hell yet (Revelation 20:13 says the final judgment hasn't happened yet)
- The soul is not described elsewhere as having a body that feels fire
- Final punishment occurs at the last judgment (Revelation 20:12-15), not immediately at death

**4. Using a parable's fictional elements as doctrinal proof violates sound hermeneutical principles:**
We do not teach that trees literally "clap their hands" (Isaiah 55:12) because it appears in poetic passages. Parabolic/poetic language is illuminating a truth about God, not mapping geography of the afterlife.

**The principle Jesus taught**: A great reversal is coming—the rich who live in luxury while ignoring the suffering poor will face judgment. No one should wait for a miraculous sign to heed God's Word.

## Part 7: Five Theological Problems with Eternal Conscious Torment

**Problem 1: It contradicts God's stated character.**

> "God is love." (1 John 4:8)

Inflicting endless agony that achieves no redemptive purpose and never ends—even after the person has faced justice—is difficult to reconcile with the biblical picture of a loving, just God.

**Problem 2: It makes death meaningless.**

> "The wages of sin is **death**." (Romans 6:23)

If death means "you continue to exist forever in agony," then "death" is a misleading term. It no longer means what it means anywhere else in Scripture.

**Problem 3: It creates eternal evil.**

> "Now I saw a new heaven and a new earth... **God will wipe away every tear** from their eyes; there shall be **no more death, nor sorrow, nor crying. There shall be no more pain,** for the former things have passed away." (Revelation 21:1, 4)

If billions of people are suffering eternally in a parallel dimension, then Revelation 21:4 is false. There IS still sorrow, pain, and death—just somewhere else.

**Problem 4: It distorts the character of God.**

The God of Scripture is a God of proportionate justice:

> "And that servant who knew his master's will, and did not prepare himself or do according to his will, shall be beaten with **many stripes**... but he who did not know, yet committed things deserving of stripes, shall be beaten with **few.**" (Luke 12:47-48)

Punishment is proportionate. Infinite torment for finite sin is infinite punishment — disproportionate by any standard.

**Problem 5: It suggests the devil wins.**

The goal of God's plan is the **restoration of all things** and the elimination of evil:

> "...when He delivers the kingdom to God the Father, when **He puts an end to all rule and all authority and power... The last enemy that will be destroyed is death.**" (1 Corinthians 15:24, 26)

If evil-doers exist eternally (even in torment), then evil and suffering are never truly ended.

## Part 8: The Historical Origin of Eternal Torment Doctrine

**Source**: Greek and Roman mythology — Tartarus, Hades, Elysium (the underworld realms with their associated punishments appeared in Homer's *Odyssey*, Hesiod's *Theogony*, and Plato's *Republic*).

**Entry into Christianity**:
- **Tertullian** (~160–225 CE): began applying Greek concepts of eternal torment to Christian eschatology
- **Augustine** (~354–430 CE): fully systematized eternal torment as Christian doctrine
- **Council of Florence (1439 CE)**: formally defined the doctrine

The early Hebrew concept (Sheol = the grave) was progressively replaced by the Greek/Roman concept of a conscious underworld.

## Conclusion: The God of Scripture

The God of the Bible is not a cosmic torturer. He is a God of love and perfect justice—whose justice results in complete, final, irrevocable punishment (not endless suffering), and whose redemption results in complete, permanent, uninterrupted life.

The wicked face destruction—thorough, permanent, irreversible. The righteous receive life—eternal, joyful, unending.

This is consistent with:
- God's character (love and justice)
- God's language (death means death)
- God's promise (no more pain in the new creation)
- The redemptive purpose of judgment (God removes evil, not perpetuates it)

> **"And God will wipe away every tear from their eyes; there shall be no more death, nor sorrow, nor crying. There shall be no more pain, for the former things have passed away."** (Revelation 21:4)

This promise is only fully kept if evil—and those who chose it irrevocably—are fully, permanently ended. Not endlessly sustained in agony.

> **"The wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord."** (Romans 6:23)
