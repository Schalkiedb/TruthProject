## A Bible-Only Study

## Introduction: The Question of Forgiveness

Forgiveness of sins is the most vital transaction in existence. Who grants it, how it is obtained, and what ensures it are questions with eternal weight. The Catholic Church teaches that:

1. Sins must be confessed to an ordained priest, who then grants absolution (forgiveness) by the authority of his ordination
2. Even after absolution, a "temporal penalty" remains — which can be reduced or eliminated through **indulgences** (assigned acts, pilgrimages, or historically, payment)

This study examines both doctrines against Scripture's clear teaching on forgiveness and the priesthood.

---

## Part 1: Indulgences — A Definition and History

### 1.1 What Is an Indulgence?

**Catholic Catechism (CCC 1471)**: "An indulgence is a remission before God of the temporal punishment due to sins whose guilt has already been forgiven... The faithful Christian who is duly disposed gains an indulgence under prescribed conditions through the action of the Church..."

The structure:
1. Mortal sin is confessed and absolved by a priest (guilt removed)
2. A "temporal punishment" is still owed — to be served in purgatory after death
3. An indulgence reduces or eliminates this temporal punishment
4. Indulgences are obtained through specific acts: prayers, pilgrimages, certain works, or historically — money

### 1.2 History — How They Led to the Reformation

By the 16th century, the sale of indulgences had become a massive fundraising mechanism for the Catholic Church. A Dominican friar, **Johann Tetzel**, was selling "full indulgences" throughout Germany with the famous marketing slogan:

*"When the coin in the coffer rings, the soul from purgatory springs."*

You paid money. Your deceased relative was released from purgatory. This was the official position of the Church.

**Martin Luther** nailed his Ninety-Five Theses to the church door at Wittenberg on **October 31, 1517 CE** — specifically targeting the sale of indulgences. The Reformation began here.

**Key Historical Facts**:
- Indulgences were formally defined at the **Council of Trent (1563 CE)**
- They were **not abolished** — only the most obvious abuses of direct sale
- The Catholic Church still **issues indulgences** today (Plenary Indulgences, Partial Indulgences, etc.)
- Pope Benedict XVI granted a Plenary Indulgence for visiting St. Peter's during the Year of the Priest (2009–2010)

## Part 2: Indulgences — The Biblical Refutation

### 2.1 Christ's Sacrifice Removed ALL Penalty, Not Just Guilt

The entire premise of indulgences rests on a distinction between "guilt" (removed at absolution) and "temporal punishment" (still owed after absolution). Scripture knows no such distinction.

> "But this Man [Christ], after He had offered **one sacrifice for sins forever**, sat down at the right hand of God." (Hebrews 10:12)

> "For by **one offering He has perfected forever** those who are being sanctified." (Hebrews 10:14)

"Perfected forever" — entirely, completely, permanently. Not "partially cleansed, with a residual temporal penalty."

> "...the blood of Jesus Christ His Son **cleanses us from all sin**." (1 John 1:7)

**ALL sin.** Not "all guilt, but not all temporal consequence." The blood of Christ cleanses the whole — because Christ bore the complete penalty:

> "Surely He has borne our griefs and carried our sorrows... He was wounded for our transgressions, He was bruised for our iniquities; the chastisement **for our peace** was upon Him, and **by His stripes we are healed**." (Isaiah 53:4-5)

> "Christ has redeemed us from **the curse of the law**, having become a curse for us." (Galatians 3:13)

**The entire curse — not part of it.**

### 2.2 Salvation Is Not for Sale

> "...knowing that you were not redeemed with corruptible things, like **silver or gold**... but with the **precious blood of Christ**." (1 Peter 1:18-19)

Any system that monetizes the forgiveness or release from penalty of sin is directly contradicting this verse. The blood of Christ purchased redemption. Silver and gold cannot add to or supplement what Christ's blood already fully accomplished.

> "**Freely you have received, freely give.**" (Matthew 10:8)

> "The gift of God is **eternal life** in Christ Jesus our Lord." (Romans 6:23)

A **gift** cannot be purchased. An indulgence is precisely that — a purchased product. The two are mutually exclusive.

## Part 3: Confession to a Priest — The Biblical Examination

### 3.1 What the Catholic Church Teaches

**Catholic Catechism (CCC 1461)**: "Bishops and priests, by virtue of the sacrament of Holy Orders, have the power to forgive all sins..."

**Catholic Catechism (CCC 1456)**: "The confession of sins, even from a simply human point of view, frees us and facilitates our reconciliation with others. Through such an admission man looks squarely at the sins he is guilty of, takes responsibility for them, and thereby opens himself again to God and to the communion of the Church."

**Catholic Canon Law (Canon 960)**: "Individual and integral confession and absolution constitute the only ordinary means by which a member of the faithful conscious of grave sin is reconciled with God and the Church."

The priest must absolve — this is "the only ordinary means."

### 3.2 Who Can Forgive Sins? Scripture's Answer

Jesus's contemporaries gave the correct answer when they saw Him forgiving sins:

> "And some of the scribes were sitting there and reasoning in their hearts, '**Why does this Man speak blasphemies like this? Who can forgive sins but God alone?**'" (Mark 2:7)

This is the biblical answer, stated by those who understood Jewish theology perfectly: **God alone forgives sins.** Their error was not in this statement — it was in not recognizing that Jesus was God.

> "If we confess our sins, **He [God] is faithful and just to forgive us our sins** and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness." (1 John 1:9)

When John says "if we confess our sins" — the pronoun "HE" who forgives is God. John does not say "go to a priest and confess." He says: confess, and **He (God)** forgives.

> "...her sins, which are many, **are forgiven**, for she loved much..." [Jesus, directly to the sinful woman] (Luke 7:47-48)

> "Who is this who **even forgives sins?**" (Luke 7:49 — the crowd's response)

Jesus forgave sins directly — not through any secondary priestly system. His authority to forgive came from His divine nature, not from institutional appointment.

### 3.3 The ONE Mediator Principle

> "For there is **one God and one Mediator** between God and men, the Man Christ Jesus." (1 Timothy 2:5)

A priest claiming to mediate forgiveness between a sinner and God places himself in the role Scripture assigns exclusively to Christ.

### 3.4 What Jesus Meant in John 20:23

> "If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them; if you retain the sins of any, they are retained." (John 20:23)

This is the proof text used to support priestly absolution. However, the context and the rest of Scripture clarify:

**Context**: Jesus had just breathed on the disciples and said "Receive the Holy Spirit." He was commissioning them to preach the gospel — the message by which sins are forgiven or retained (through acceptance or rejection of the gospel).

**Understanding**: The verb "forgiven" (*aphientai*) in Greek is in the perfect passive — "have been forgiven." When disciples preach the gospel and someone believes, those sins *have been* forgiven (by God, through Christ's blood). When someone rejects the gospel, sins are retained. The disciples *declare* the terms of forgiveness — they do not *grant* forgiveness by their pronouncement.

This is identical to: "He who believes and is baptized will be saved; but he who does not believe will be condemned" (Mark 16:16) — the preacher declares the terms; God executes the result.

## Part 4: Where Scripture Points for Confession and Forgiveness

### 4.1 Confess Directly to God

> "Have mercy on me, O God... **Wash me thoroughly from my iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin. For I acknowledge my transgressions, and my sin is always before me. Against You, You only, have I sinned.**" (Psalm 51:1-4 — David's confession, directly to God)

David's model confession is addressed entirely to God — no priest, no intermediary.

> "I acknowledged my sin to You, and my iniquity I have not hidden. I said, 'I will confess my transgressions to the LORD,' and **You forgave the iniquity of my sin.**" (Psalm 32:5)

Confess to God → God forgives. Directly. No priest.

### 4.2 Confess to One Another — For Healing and Accountability

> "Confess your trespasses to **one another**, and pray for one another, that you may be healed." (James 5:16)

This is the New Testament instruction for confession — **to one another** (fellow believers), not to priests. The purpose is healing and accountability, not institutional absolution. And the *result* (prayer for healing) suggests this is about practical restoration of relationships and spiritual health — not the granting of absolution.

### 4.3 Access to God Is Direct and Always Open

> "Let us therefore **come boldly to the throne of grace**, that we may obtain mercy and find grace to help in time of need." (Hebrews 4:16)

**Boldly. To the throne of grace. Directly.** No appointment needed. No priestly intermediary. No waiting room.

> "...in whom we have boldness and **access with confidence through faith in Him**." (Ephesians 3:12)

## Conclusion: The Gospel Requires No Addition

The gospel of Jesus Christ is complete, sufficient, and free:

> "In Him we have redemption through His blood, **the forgiveness of sins**, according to the riches of His grace." (Ephesians 1:7)

No temporal penalty lingers after His blood has cleansed. No priest is needed to access what Christ has already opened. No coin needs to ring for a soul to be freed.

The moment a sinner confesses directly to God, something radical happens:

> **"He [God] is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness."** (1 John 1:9)

Faithful. Just. Complete. Direct. Free.

This is the gospel. Nothing needs to be added to it.

> **"It is finished."** (John 19:30)
