# False Doctrine 5: The Mass and the Eucharist — Is Christ Re-Sacrificed?
## A Bible-Only Study

---

## Introduction: The Central Question

The Eucharist (Holy Communion, the Lord's Supper, the Mass) is the most central ritual in Catholic worship. The Catholic Church teaches that at every Mass:
- The bread and wine are transformed into the literal, physical body and blood of Jesus Christ (transubstantiation)
- The sacrificial death of Christ is "re-presented" or "made present again" — not repeated symbolically, but actually offered again

Before evaluating this claim, one question must be answered from Scripture: **Was Christ's sacrifice finished, or does it continue to be offered?**

> "It is finished." (*Tetelestai* — John 19:30)

This study examines what that Greek word means, what the Book of Hebrews teaches at length on this subject, and what the simple memorial Jesus actually instituted.

---

## Part 1: "It Is Finished" — A Legal, Complete Declaration

### 1.1 The Greek Word *Tetelestai*

> "So when Jesus had received the sour wine, He said, '**It is finished!**' And bowing His head, He gave up His spirit." (John 19:30)

The Greek word is **tetelestai** (τετέλεσται) — perfect passive indicative of *teleō*.

In the ancient world, *tetelestai* was:
- Written across paid invoices — "**PAID IN FULL**"
- Used to declare a debt completely and finally settled
- The perfect tense in Greek indicates **completed action with lasting results**—the action is done, and its effect is permanent

Jesus did not say "It is partly done." He did not say "It will be completed repeatedly." He declared: **It is finished. Paid in full. Permanently.**

## Part 2: The Book of Hebrews — The Definitive Commentary

The entire Book of Hebrews is written to contrast the inadequate, repetitive Old Covenant sacrificial system with the perfect, complete, once-for-all sacrifice of Christ. Read these passages carefully:

### 2.1 "Once for All" — Four Times in Hebrews

> "By that will we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ **once for all** [*ephapax*]." (Hebrews 10:10)

The Greek **ephapax** means "once and for all time" — decisive, unrepeatable, completely sufficient.

> "For Christ has not entered the holy places made with hands, which are copies of the true, but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us; not that He should offer Himself often, as the high priest enters the Most Holy Place every year with blood of another; **He then would have had to suffer often since the foundation of the world**; but now, once at the end of the ages, **He has appeared to put away sin by the sacrifice of Himself**." (Hebrews 9:24-26)

**If Christ were re-sacrificed at every Mass**, He would have to suffer repeatedly. This is the exact scenario Hebrews 9:26 says would have been necessary — and then says is NOT what happened. He suffered ONCE.

> "But this Man, after He had offered **one sacrifice for sins forever**, sat down at the right hand of God." (Hebrews 10:12)

**"Sat down"** is theologically significant. Old Testament priests NEVER sat down:

> "And every priest stands ministering daily and **offering repeatedly the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins**." (Hebrews 10:11)

Old Testament priests stood because their work was never done. Christ **sat down** because His work was completely finished.

### 2.2 No More Offering Needed

> "For by **one offering He has perfected forever** those who are being sanctified." (Hebrews 10:14)

> "Now where there is **remission** of these, there is **no longer an offering for sin**." (Hebrews 10:18)

"No longer an offering for sin." Scripture could not be more direct: the sacrificial system ended with Christ's death. There is **no more offering for sin** to be made.

## Part 3: The Catholic Teaching — A Careful Examination

**Catholic Catechism (CCC 1366)**: "The Eucharist is thus a sacrifice because it re-presents (makes present) the sacrifice of the cross, because it is its memorial and because it applies its fruit."

**Catholic Catechism (CCC 1367)**: "The sacrifice of Christ and the sacrifice of the Eucharist are one single sacrifice: 'The victim is one and the same: the same now offers through the ministry of priests, who then offered himself on the cross; only the manner of offering is different.'"

**Council of Trent (1545–1563 CE)**:
> "CANON 1: If any one saith, that in the Mass a true and proper sacrifice is not offered to God... let him be anathema [cursed]."
> "CANON 3: If any one saith, that the sacrifice of the mass is only a sacrifice of praise and of thanksgiving; or, that it is a bare commemoration of the sacrifice consummated on the cross, but not a propitiatory sacrifice... let him be anathema."

**The problem**: The Catholic Church curses (anathema) those who say the Mass is merely a memorial — which is the exact term Jesus used when He instituted the Supper.

## Part 4: What Jesus Actually Said When He Instituted the Supper

> "And He took bread, gave thanks and broke it, and gave it to them, saying, 'This is My body which is given for you; **do this in remembrance of Me.**' Likewise He also took the cup after supper, saying, 'This cup is the new covenant in My blood, which is shed for you.'" (Luke 22:19-20)

**"In remembrance of Me"** — Greek *anamnesis* — memorial, remembrance.

Paul repeats this structure, directly:

> "For I received from the Lord that which I also delivered to you: that the Lord Jesus on the same night in which He was betrayed took bread; and when He had given thanks, He broke it and said, 'Take, eat; this is My body which is broken for you; **do this in remembrance of Me.**' In the same manner He also took the cup after supper, saying, 'This cup is the new covenant in My blood. **Do this, as often as you drink it, in remembrance of Me.**' For **as often as you eat this bread and drink this cup, you proclaim the Lord's death till He comes.**" (1 Corinthians 11:23-26)

**Three observations**:
1. Jesus said "in remembrance of Me" — **twice** in this passage
2. Paul says we "proclaim the Lord's death" — we announce a past event, not re-enact a present sacrifice
3. "Till He comes" — the Supper looks back to the cross AND forward to the Second Coming

## Part 5: "This Is My Body" — How Did Jesus Mean It?

### 5.1 Jesus Clarified His Own Meaning in John 6

In John 6, Jesus gives the discourse on "eating His flesh and drinking His blood." Many were offended. Jesus then clarified:

> "It is the **Spirit who gives life**; the **flesh profits nothing**. The words that I speak to you **are spirit, and they are life.**" (John 6:63)

**"The flesh profits nothing."** If transubstantiation were true — if physically eating Christ's literal flesh were required for eternal life — Jesus would never say the flesh profits nothing. He explicitly says the eating is **spiritual**, equated with believing His words.

This is confirmed just before:

> "Jesus said to them, 'I am the bread of life. **He who comes to Me** shall never hunger, and **he who believes in Me** shall never thirst.'" (John 6:35)

**"Coming to Me" = "eating"; "Believing in Me" = "drinking"** — the language is figurative for faith and trust in Christ.

### 5.2 Jesus Spoke in Figures Regularly

Jesus said:
- "I am the **door**" (John 10:9)
- "I am the **vine**" (John 15:1)
- "I am the **light** of the world" (John 9:5)
- "I am the **bread** of life" (John 6:48)

None of these are intended as literal physical claims. They are powerful metaphors revealing aspects of His identity and mission.

### 5.3 Where Is Christ Physically?

> "...Jesus Christ, who has gone into heaven and **is at the right hand of God**, with angels and authorities and powers having been subjected to Him." (1 Peter 3:21-22)

> "So then, after the Lord had spoken to them, He was **received up into heaven, and sat down at the right hand of God.**" (Mark 16:19)

> "And Stephen, full of the Holy Spirit, gazed into heaven and saw the glory of God, and **Jesus standing at the right hand of God.**" (Acts 7:55-56)

> "...whom heaven must **receive until the times of restoration of all things**." (Acts 3:21)

Christ's physical body is IN HEAVEN — not being multiplied across thousands of Catholic altars simultaneously. Transubstantiation requires Christ's physical body to be in many places at once, which contradicts every passage showing His physical location as the Father's right hand.

## Part 6: The Origin of Transubstantiation

**It was not the teaching of the early church.**

### Timeline:
- **John 19:30** (33 CE): Christ declares "It is finished"
- **1 Corinthians 11** (~55 CE): Paul teaches the Supper as a memorial proclamation
- **Early church** (1st–3rd centuries): Supper understood as memorial and thanksgiving (*Eucharistia* = "thanksgiving")
- **Paschasius Radbertus** (831 CE): First theologian to formally argue for a literal, physical change
- **Fourth Lateran Council (1215 CE)**: Transubstantiation officially defined as doctrine
- **Council of Trent (1545–1563 CE)**: Doctrine defended against Reformation critiques; anyone denying it is "anathema"

**Aristotelian Philosophy**: The word "transubstantiation" comes from Aristotle's concepts of *substance* (what something fundamentally is) and *accidents* (what something appears to be). The doctrine teaches that the accidents (appearance, taste, smell) remain bread and wine, while the substance changes to Christ's body and blood. This is Aristotelian metaphysics, not Hebrew theology.

### Pagan Parallels:
The eating of a god's flesh and blood was common in ancient mystery religions:
- **Dionysian mysteries**: Eating of Dionysus's flesh in ritual meals
- **Mithraic cult**: Sacred ritual meal with bread and water (or wine)
- **Egyptian Osiris worship**: Ritual consumption related to Osiris's death and resurrection

These parallels do not prove causation, but they demonstrate the concept was widely available in Greco-Roman culture when the doctrine developed.

## Part 8: The Priesthood — Who Has the Right to Offer Sacrifice?

The Catholic Mass requires ordained priests who "have the power to bring Christ down from heaven" (a phrase used in Catholic teaching) and offer Him again.

**Scripture on the Priesthood**:

> "For there is **one God and one Mediator** between God and men, the Man Christ Jesus." (1 Timothy 2:5)

> "But this Man [Christ], because He continues forever, has an **unchangeable priesthood**." (Hebrews 7:24)

> "Therefore He is also able to save to the uttermost those who come to God **through Him, since He always lives to make intercession for them.**" (Hebrews 7:25)

> "You also, as living stones, are being built up a **spiritual house, a holy priesthood**, to offer up spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ." (1 Peter 2:5)

**Conclusion**: 
1. Christ has an unchangeable, permanent priesthood — no human can assume it
2. Every believer is part of a "royal priesthood" (1 Peter 2:9) — not to offer Christ again, but to offer "spiritual sacrifices"
3. One mediator only — no priestly system stands between believers and direct access to God through Christ

## Part 9: What the Lord's Supper Actually Accomplishes

The biblical Supper is a powerful, meaningful act that:

**1. Proclaims the Lord's death**:
> "For as often as you eat this bread and drink this cup, **you proclaim the Lord's death till He comes.**" (1 Corinthians 11:26)

**2. Brings believers into communion (fellowship) with Christ**:
> "The cup of blessing which we bless, is it not the **communion of the blood of Christ**? The bread which we break, is it not the **communion of the body of Christ**?" (1 Corinthians 10:16)

**3. Points forward to the kingdom**:
> "But I say to you, I will not drink of this fruit of the vine from now on until that day when I drink it new with you **in My Father's kingdom.**" (Matthew 26:29)

**4. Calls for self-examination**:
> "But let a man **examine himself**, and so let him eat of the bread and drink of the cup." (1 Corinthians 11:28)

## Conclusion

The Bible leaves no ambiguity on these points:
- Christ's sacrifice was offered **once**, is **complete**, and needs **no repetition** (Hebrews 10:10-18)
- The Supper Jesus instituted is a **memorial** and **proclamation** — not a re-sacrifice (1 Corinthians 11:23-26; Luke 22:19-20)
- Christ's body is physically in **heaven** at God's right hand — not multiplied across altars (Acts 3:21; Acts 7:55)
- There is **one Mediator** and **one High Priest** — the living, interceding Christ (1 Timothy 2:5; Hebrews 7:24-25)
- The eating and drinking of John 6 are **spiritual** — equated with believing in Christ, not literal consumption (John 6:35, 63)

The words of Christ stand as the clearest possible guide:

> **"Do this in remembrance of Me."** (Luke 22:19)

Not "do this to offer Me again." Not "do this to receive Me literally." Simply: **Remember Me.**
