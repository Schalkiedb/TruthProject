# False Doctrine 12: Praying to Saints — Seeking the Dead for Intercession
## A Bible-Only Study

---

## Introduction: Both Sides of the Same Problem

Praying to saints combines two separately condemned practices:

1. **Seeking communication with the dead** — explicitly classified as an abomination (Deuteronomy 18:10-12)
2. **Seeking intercession through any mediator other than Christ** — directly contradicted by 1 Timothy 2:5

The practice seems devotional and sincere. Many who engage in it genuinely love God and mean to draw closer to Him through holy people they admire. But sincerity does not determine what God has commanded. This study applies Scripture to each claim that supports praying to saints.

> "There is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the way of death." (Proverbs 14:12)

---

## Part 1: God's Absolute Prohibition on Communication with the Dead

This is not a minor principle or a debated point. It is one of the clearest, most categorical prohibitions in the entire Bible — given directly by God, repeated multiple times, classified alongside the worst forms of occult practice.

> "There shall not be found among you anyone who makes his son or his daughter pass through the fire, or one who practices witchcraft, or a soothsayer, or one who interprets omens, or a sorcerer, or one who conjures spells, or **a medium, or a spiritist, or one who calls up the dead**. For **all who do these things are an abomination to the LORD**, and because of these abominations the LORD your God drives them out from before you." (Deuteronomy 18:10-12)

**Key observation**: "One who calls up the dead" (*doresh el-ha-meitim* in Hebrew = literally "one who seeks/inquires of the dead") is placed in the same category as witchcraft, sorcery, and necromancy — and called an **abomination**.

Praying to deceased saints is, by this definition, "seeking/inquiring of the dead" — regardless of what the deceased person is called (saint, apostle, Mary, etc.).

> "And when they say to you, 'Seek those who are mediums and wizards, who whisper and mutter,' should not a people **seek their God**? Should they **seek the dead on behalf of the living**?" (Isaiah 8:19)

Isaiah frames it as an either/or: either you seek the living God, or you seek the dead. He makes the answer obvious — the living God, not the dead.

> "For the living know that they will die; but **the dead know nothing**, and they have no more reward, for the memory of them is forgotten." (Ecclesiastes 9:5)

**The dead know nothing.** They are not aware. They cannot hear prayers. They cannot intercede. Any response to appeals made to the dead would therefore come from something other than the deceased — Scripture identifies what that "something" is.

---

## Part 2: If the Dead Cannot Hear, Who Answers?

This is the crucial question. If deceased saints are unconscious (as established in File 02), they cannot hear prayers directed to them. So what or who does the invoker interact with when they feel their prayers to saints are being answered?

Scripture is clear on the existence of deceptive spiritual entities:

> "Now the Spirit expressly says that in latter times some will depart from the faith, giving heed to **deceiving spirits** and **doctrines of demons**." (1 Timothy 4:1)

> "And no wonder! For **Satan himself transforms himself into an angel of light**. Therefore it is no great thing if his ministers also transform themselves into ministers of righteousness." (2 Corinthians 11:14-15)

> "For they are **spirits of demons**, performing signs, which go out to the kings of the earth and of the whole world, to gather them to the battle of that great day of God Almighty." (Revelation 16:14)

Satan and his agents can produce manifestations, experiences, and apparent answers that feel entirely authentic. The prohibition against seeking the dead is not because nothing happens — it is because what happens is not from God, and not from the deceased. It is from deceptive spiritual sources.

This is precisely why God's prohibition is so absolute: the danger is not that nothing will respond, but that something **will** — and it will not be what the person believes it to be.

---

## Part 3: One Mediator — The Most Direct Biblical Refutation

> "For there is **one God and one Mediator between God and men, the Man Christ Jesus**, who gave Himself a ransom for all..." (1 Timothy 2:5-6)

The word **one** (*heis*) is singular, absolute, exclusive. Paul did not say "one primary mediator alongside others." He said one.

This verse makes the entire system of saintly intercession impossible from a biblical standpoint:
- If there is one mediator, there cannot also be 10,000 saints acting as sub-mediators
- If Christ is THE mediator, Mary cannot be a co-mediatrix
- If Christ alone bridges the gap between God and humanity, no deceased person — however holy — can stand in that place

> "Therefore He is also able to save to the uttermost those who come to God through Him, since **He always lives to make intercession for them.**" (Hebrews 7:25)

Christ's intercession is:
- **Continuous** ("always lives")
- **Perfectly effective** ("to the uttermost")
- **Present** ("makes intercession" — present tense, ongoing)

There is no deficiency in Christ's intercession that requires supplementing with appeals to saints.

---

## Part 4: Direct Access to God for Every Believer

The entire New Covenant is built on the premise of **direct access** to God for every believer — not access mediated through a chain of holy people.

> "Therefore, brethren, **having boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus**, by a new and living way which He consecrated for us, through the veil... let us **draw near** with a true heart in full assurance of faith." (Hebrews 10:19-22)

**Every believer** has bold, direct access into the presence of God — through Christ's blood, not through saints' intercession.

> "Let us therefore **come boldly to the throne of grace**, that we may obtain mercy and find grace to help in time of need." (Hebrews 4:16)

**Come boldly** — directly, confidently, personally. No intermediary saints required or permitted.

> "...in whom we have **boldness and access with confidence through faith in Him**." (Ephesians 3:12)

The tearing of the temple veil at Christ's death (Matthew 27:51) symbolized exactly this: the barrier between God and people was permanently removed. Direct access was established. No human priest, no saint, no Mary stands in the gap — because there is no longer a gap.

---

## Part 5: The Example of the Apostles — They Never Claimed Ongoing Intercession

If praying to deceased saints were valid, we would expect:
1. Jesus to teach it
2. The apostles to practice it
3. The New Testament epistles to instruct it

**None of these occur.** Not once. Ever.

When the early church faced persecution, imprisonment, and need, they prayed **to God directly**:

> "Lord, look on their threats, and grant to Your servants that with all boldness they may speak Your word... And when they had prayed, the place where they were assembled together was shaken; and they were all filled with the Holy Spirit." (Acts 4:29, 31)

They didn't pray to Peter, James, Stephen, or any deceased holy person. They prayed to **God directly**.

When Paul requested prayer, he asked **living** believers to pray for him:

> "...praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit... and for me, that utterance may be given to me, that I may open my mouth boldly to make known the mystery of the gospel." (Ephesians 6:18-19)

**Living believers praying for each other** — this is biblical intercession. Not prayers directed to the deceased.

---

## Part 6: The Biblical Role of the Dead — In God's Hands, Not Standing By to Hear

Where are the dead? Not standing at a heavenly switchboard awaiting prayer. As established in File 02 (State of the Dead):

> "For the living know that they will die; but **the dead know nothing**." (Ecclesiastes 9:5)

> "His spirit departs, he returns to his earth; **in that very day his plans perish.**" (Psalm 146:4)

> "Do not put your trust in princes, nor in a son of man, in whom there is no help." (Psalm 146:3)

The dead are in God's care, sleeping in the grave, awaiting the resurrection. They are not conscious, not monitoring the prayers of the living, and not in a position to relay petitions to God.

Even King Saul's desperate attempt to contact the dead prophet Samuel (1 Samuel 28) is presented by Scripture as a serious sin — not as a viable spiritual practice. God condemned Saul for it:

> "So Saul died for his unfaithfulness which he had committed against the LORD, because he did not keep the word of the LORD, and also **because he consulted a medium for guidance**." (1 Chronicles 10:13)

## Part 7: The Historical Development of Praying to Saints

### Timeline:
- **1st–3rd century CE**: Early Christians honored martyrs at their tombs — but not as intercessors. They prayed FOR the dead (and this practice itself is unbiblical — see purgatory refutation in File 02), not TO the dead
- **4th century CE**: As Christianity became the Roman state religion and pagans converted in large numbers, the cult of the saints absorbed practices formerly directed at pagan gods and heroes. Local pagan deities were gradually replaced by local patron saints
- **Specific parallels**:
  - Venus's sacred sites → Mary's shrines
  - Neptune, god of the sea → prayers to a patron saint for sailors
  - Local healing gods → healing saints with specialized domains
- **Medieval period**: The practice became fully systematized; the calendar of saints became an extensive devotional framework
- **Council of Trent (1563 CE)**: Formally affirmed the invocation of saints against Protestant objection

**The practice entered Christianity from paganism** — the worship of patron gods/heroes was domesticated into saintly intercession without changing its essential character.

## Conclusion: The Living God Waits to Be Approached

The practice of praying to saints places a chain of human intermediaries between the believer and God — a chain God never established and explicitly dismantled through Christ's death. It simultaneously invokes the dead, which God has categorically forbidden. And it diminishes the perfect, always-available, always-effective intercession Christ is performing right now on behalf of every believer.

The biblical invitation is breathtaking in its directness:

> **"Let us therefore come boldly to the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy and find grace to help in time of need."** (Hebrews 4:16)

You. Boldly. Directly. To the throne of grace. Now.

No saint in a grave can give you what a living Savior at God's right hand offers freely and continuously.

> **"For through Him we both have access by one Spirit to the Father."** (Ephesians 2:18)

Access. Through Christ. To the Father. For every believer. Always.
