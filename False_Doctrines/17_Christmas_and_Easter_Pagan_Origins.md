Before examining the historical evidence, two biblical questions frame this study:

> "Take heed to yourself that you are **not ensnared to follow** them [pagan nations]... and that you do not inquire after their gods, saying, 'How did these nations serve their gods? **God explicitly forbids adopting pagan worship methods and redirecting them toward Him.** The justification "but we're doing it for God now" does not appear in Scripture as an acceptable defense.

With these principles established, the historical evidence speaks for itself.

## Part 1: Christmas — The December 25 Date

### 1.1 What Scripture Actually Says About Jesus's Birth

The Bible does not state the date of Jesus's birth. No month, no day, no season. The absence of a date in Scripture is itself significant — God chose not to record it.

What Scripture *does* say about the circumstances provides important clues:

> "Now there were in the same country **shepherds living out in the fields, keeping watch over their flock by night**." (Luke 2:8)

**Shepherds in the fields at night** in ancient Judea followed a seasonal pattern. During the cold winter months (mid-November through February), shepherds brought their flocks into sheltered enclosures. Grazing in open fields at night was a spring and summer activity. Most historians and biblical scholars conclude that Jesus was likely born somewhere between **spring and early autumn** — not in December.

The Encyclopedia Britannica acknowledges:
> "The precise origin of assigning December 25 as the birth date of Jesus is unclear. The New Testament provides no clues in this regard."

### 1.2 The Roman Festival of Saturnalia

**Saturnalia** was the Roman festival honoring Saturn, god of agriculture. It was celebrated annually from **December 17 to December 23**, featuring:
- Gift-giving
- Feasting and revelry
- Role reversals (masters served slaves)
- Decoration of homes with greenery

### 1.3 Sol Invictus — The Sun God's Birthday

**December 25** was officially declared the birthday of ***Sol Invictus*** (the Unconquered Sun) by Roman Emperor **Aurelian in 274 CE** — approximately 274 years after Christ's birth.

Sol Invictus was a syncretic sun deity popular in the Roman Empire's late period. December 25 marked the winter solstice (by the Julian calendar), when days begin lengthening — the "rebirth" of the sun.

**The Mithras connection**: The Persian deity **Mithras**, worshipped widely among Roman soldiers, was also associated with December 25 as his birthday. Mithraism involved:
- Birth of a divine figure from a virgin/rock
- Adoration by shepherds
- Sacred meal
- December 25 birthday celebrations

### 1.4 The Church's Adoption of December 25

- **Eusebius and Origen** (3rd century CE): Neither mentions December 25 as Christ's birth date
- **First recorded December 25 celebration**: 336 CE, in Rome — under Constantine's reign (the same emperor who synthesized paganism and Christianity)
- **Pope Julius I**: Officially declared December 25 as Christmas in the mid-4th century
- The Catholic Encyclopedia acknowledges: "The reason why the fathers of the Church selected December 25 as the date for the Nativity... **because this was the date of the pagan feast of the birth of the Sol Invictus**"

God's warning applies directly:

> "Thus says the LORD: '**Do not learn the way of the Gentiles**... For the customs of the peoples are futile; for one cuts a tree from the forest, the work of the hands of the craftsman with an ax. They decorate it with silver and gold; they fasten it with nails and hammers so that it will not topple.'" (Jeremiah 10:2-4)

**The decorated tree** — Jeremiah describes the practice of cutting trees, bringing them indoors, and decorating them with silver and gold. This is identified as a Gentile/pagan custom and explicitly prohibited.

### 1.5 Other Christmas Elements — Their Pagan Origins

## Part 2: Easter — The Spring Festival

### 2.1 The Name Itself Is Not in the Bible

The word "Easter" appears once in the KJV Bible (Acts 12:4) — and it is a **mistranslation**. The Greek word is **pascha** (πάσχα) = **Passover**. Every major translation (NIV, ESV, NASB, RSV, NRSV) correctly translates it as Passover.

The name "Easter" derives from:
- **Old English** *Ēostre* or *Ēastre* — a Germanic spring goddess
- **Germanic** *Ostara* — the dawn/spring goddess whose festival was celebrated at the spring equinox
- Documented by the venerable Bede (~720 CE) in *De Temporum Ratione*

### 2.2 The Babylonian and Assyrian Connection

The spring fertility goddess appears across multiple ancient cultures under related names:
- **Ishtar** (Babylonian/Assyrian) — goddess of fertility, love, war; spring festival with eggs and rabbits (fertility symbols)
- **Astarte** (Canaanite) — condemned repeatedly in Scripture as pagan worship Israel was forbidden to follow (1 Kings 11:5, 33; 2 Kings 23:13)
- **Ashtoreth** — "Then the children of Israel again did evil in the sight of the LORD... they served the Baals and the Ashtoreths" (Judges 10:6)

**Eggs and rabbits** as Easter symbols: both are ancient symbols of fertility associated with spring goddess festivals — connecting directly to Ishtar/Astarte worship.

### 2.3 The Sunrise Easter Service

> "So He brought me into the inner court of the LORD's house; and there, at the door of the temple of the LORD, between the porch and the altar, were about twenty-five men with their backs toward the temple of the LORD and their faces toward the east, and they were **worshiping the sun toward the east**. Then He said to me, 'Have you seen this, O son of man? Is it a trivial thing to the house of Judah to commit the abominations which they commit here?'" (Ezekiel 8:16-17)

**Facing east to worship the rising sun** — God called this an abomination IN HIS OWN TEMPLE. The Easter Sunday sunrise service faces east to celebrate the rising of the sun on the spring morning — a practice that mirrors exactly what God condemned in Ezekiel 8.

### 2.4 What Did the Early Church Observe?

The early Church — particularly Jewish believers — observed **Passover** (*Pascha*), not Easter:

> "For indeed Christ, our **Passover**, was sacrificed for us." (1 Corinthians 5:7)

Paul's explicit identification: Christ is our Passover Lamb. The fulfillment of Passover is the proper memorial of Christ's death and resurrection — already embedded in God's appointed calendar.

The **Quartodeciman controversy** (2nd century CE) was a dispute between those who celebrated Christ's death on *14 Nisan* (Passover date) and those who celebrated on the Sunday after the spring equinox. The Sunday celebration (Easter) ultimately won politically, aided significantly by anti-Jewish sentiment in the Church.

**Council of Nicaea (325 CE)**: Constantine used the council to formally separate Christian practice from Jewish Passover, mandating Sunday Easter for all churches. His letter to churches after Nicaea reveals the motivation:

> "It appeared an unworthy thing that in the celebration of this most holy feast we should follow the practice of the Jews, who have **defiled their hands with enormous sin**... We ought not, therefore, to have anything in common with the Jews." — Constantine, Epistle to the Churches

The date change was driven not by Scripture but by imperial anti-Semitism.

## Part 3: God's Appointed Times — Already Given in Scripture

God did not leave His people without appointed times for worship. He established them in Scripture:

> "These are the **feasts of the LORD**, holy convocations which you shall proclaim at their appointed times." (Leviticus 23:2)

The biblical feasts include:
- **Passover** (14 Nisan) — death of the Lamb (fulfilled in Christ's crucifixion)
- **Feast of Unleavened Bread** — cleansing from sin
- **Firstfruits** — first of the harvest (fulfilled in Christ's resurrection — "the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep," 1 Corinthians 15:20)
- **Pentecost** — outpouring of the Spirit (fulfilled in Acts 2)

**Christ's death and resurrection are already commemorated in God's own appointed festivals** — on the very days they historically occurred. God built the memorial into His calendar in advance.

Scripture commands believers to celebrate these:

> "For the LORD your God will bless you in all your harvest and in all the work of your hands, and your **joy will be complete**. Three times a year all your males shall appear before the LORD your God in the place which He chooses." (Deuteronomy 16:15-16)

## Part 4: The Principle — God's Way vs. Man's Innovation

### The Cain Principle (Genesis 4)

Cain brought an offering to God — sincere, from his own work. Abel brought what God had specified. God accepted Abel's and rejected Cain's. The lesson: **sincerity does not determine acceptability — obedience to God's specified method does.**

### Nadab and Abihu (Leviticus 10)

> "Then Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, each took his censer and put fire in it, put incense on it, and offered **profane fire before the LORD, which He had not commanded them**. So fire went out from the LORD and devoured them." (Leviticus 10:1-2)

They offered to God — but in a way "He had not commanded them." God had specified how. They improvised. The result was catastrophic.

The principle: **God's stated method matters.** Worship offered in sincerity outside of God's revealed method is "profane" — even when directed toward the true God.

### Jesus's Standard

> "But in vain they worship Me, teaching as doctrines the commandments of men." (Matthew 15:9)

**Vain** = empty, profitless, achieving nothing.

## Conclusion: What God Has Already Given

This is perhaps the most practically challenging study in this series — because Christmas and Easter are deeply emotionally meaningful to hundreds of millions of sincere Christians. The challenge is not to condemn sincere people, but to ask honestly: **Is what we are doing what God asked for, or what we chose?**

God gave:
- **The Passover** — to remember Christ's sacrifice (fulfilled on the exact Passover date)
- **Firstfruits** — to remember His resurrection (fulfilled on the exact Firstfruits date)
- **The Sabbath** — to remember the Creator who rested on the seventh day

Man introduced:
- **December 25** — borrowed from Sol Invictus and Saturnalia
- **Easter Sunday** — named after a pagan goddess, dated by the spring equinox, moving the memorial from Passover

God's Word set the standard from the beginning:

> **"You shall not worship the LORD your God in that way [the way of the Gentiles]."** (Deuteronomy 12:4, 31)

The question for every sincere believer is not: "Do I love God?" Of course you do. The question is: "Am I willing to worship Him according to His revealed Word rather than inherited human tradition?"

That answer requires honesty, courage, and a heart that places Truth above comfort.

> **"God is Spirit, and those who worship Him must worship in spirit and truth."** (John 4:24)
